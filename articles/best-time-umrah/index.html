Best Time to Perform Umrah — Months, Weather & Crowds Guide

Umrah can be performed at almost any time of the year — unlike Hajj, it is not tied to fixed days. But "any time" is not "all times are equal": Makkah's weather, the crowds in the Haram and hotel prices swing enormously from month to month. The best time for you depends on what you value most — mild weather, spiritual intensity, quiet, or cost. Here is the honest month-by-month picture.

The reward never sleeps: The Prophet ﷺ said: "Umrah in Ramadan is equal to Hajj" — and in another narration, "equal to Hajj with me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1782; Sahih Muslim 1256). And he ﷺ said: "Umrah to the next Umrah is an expiation for whatever (sins) are between them" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1773; Sahih Muslim 1349) — so every season carries its own blessing.

The best weather window — November to February: These are Makkah's mild months. Daytime temperatures sit around 25–30°C, evenings are cool enough for a light layer, and Tawaf and the walk between the Haram and your hotel stay comfortable even for the elderly. If your group includes parents, grandparents or small children, plan for this window first. The one caution: late December brings school holidays worldwide, so crowds grow and prices jump even though the weather is at its best.

Ramadan — the greatest reward, the greatest crowds: Because an Umrah in Ramadan carries the reward described above, Makkah fills to its limits — above all in the last ten nights, when the Haram stays packed through the night for Qiyam and the search for Laylatul Qadr. Hotels near the Haram reach their highest prices of the year, and permits and good rooms go early. Go in Ramadan for the reward with your eyes open: book months ahead, pace yourself around fasting, and read our Umrah in Ramadan guide before you commit.

Summer — May to August, handle with care: Makkah's summer heat regularly reaches 40–45°C and beyond at midday. Tawaf on the open Mataf and the walk to the hotel become genuinely hard, especially for the elderly, children and anyone with health conditions. If summer is your only option: perform Tawaf in the early morning or at night, carry water constantly, rest in the heat of the day, and pack exactly as our Umrah Packing List describes for hot weather. The compensation is real, though — outside the school holidays, summer weeks can be quieter and cheaper.

The Hajj closure — Umrah stops: There is one season when Umrah is simply not available: as Hajj approaches, Umrah visa issuing winds down, existing Umrah pilgrims are required to leave, and Umrah permits pause so the holy cities can receive the Hajj pilgrims. The Umrah season then reopens after Hajj, in the new Islamic year. Never plan an Umrah that touches the Hajj days themselves — see our Hajj 2027 guide for the season's dates and how the two pilgrimages differ.

School holidays — the hidden crowd driver: Whenever schools close in the big sending countries — late December to early January, spring breaks, and the long summer holidays — family Umrah trips surge, Haram crowds swell, and prices follow. If your dates are flexible, travelling in term-time weeks is the single easiest way to find a calmer, cheaper Umrah. Families travelling in those peak weeks should plan with our Umrah for First-Timers guide and book everything early.

Month-by-month, in short:

  • Muharram – Safar (season reopens after Hajj): The quietest Haram of the year at first, but still hot — a good pick for heat-tolerant pilgrims who want space and lower prices.
  • Rabi al-Awwal – Jumada al-Akhirah (roughly autumn into early winter): Cooling steadily, moderate crowds — for many, the sweet spot of the whole year.
  • Rajab – Sha'ban: Mild weather and a spiritual build-up towards Ramadan; crowds grow week by week as Ramadan nears.
  • Ramadan: Maximum reward, maximum crowds and prices — book far ahead, especially for the last ten nights.
  • Shawwal: A post-Ramadan lull with warm but manageable weather; often good value.
  • Dhul Qa'dah – Dhul Hijjah: The season winds down and then closes for Hajj — do not plan Umrah here.

So, when should you go? For weather and comfort: November to February. For reward: Ramadan, accepting the crowds. For quiet and value: the weeks just after the season reopens, or term-time weeks in autumn. Travelling from Pakistan? Our Umrah from Pakistan guide adds the flights, visa and package details for your route. Whichever month you choose, an Umrah performed sincerely is never wasted — the calendar only changes the conditions, not the acceptance.

Quick questions:

  • Which is the least crowded time for Umrah? The weeks right after the Umrah season reopens following Hajj, and term-time weeks in mid-January to February, are usually the quietest — though "quiet" in Makkah is always relative.
  • Can I perform Umrah during Hajj season? No. Umrah visas and permits pause as Hajj approaches, and Umrah pilgrims must leave before Hajj begins. Umrah resumes after Hajj, in the new Islamic year.
  • What is the best time for elderly parents? November to February, when Makkah's weather is mildest. Avoid the summer heat and, if possible, the peak crowds of Ramadan's last ten nights.
