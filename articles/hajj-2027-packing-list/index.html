Hajj 2027 Packing List for First-Timers: What to Pack for Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah

Hajj 2027 is expected around mid May 2027, with the Day of Arafat most likely on 15 May 2027 subject to moon sighting. First-timers usually overpack, then carry too much through the five days of Hajj. The fix is a three bag system: one main suitcase left at the hotel, one small day backpack for Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah, and one secure pouch worn on the body for documents, phone and cash. See our Hajj 2027 Registration guide and the days of Hajj for the full picture.

The three bag system: Bag one is the main suitcase. It stays at your hotel with everything not needed during the five days: spare clothes, gifts and anything heavy. Bag two is the day backpack, small and light enough to carry all day, holding what you need for Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah. Bag three is the body pouch, worn on your body for documents, phone and cash. Hajj means long walks, crowded buses and nights away from your hotel, so one heavy bag makes every ritual harder and buries your documents when you need them most.

Documents and money: These live in the body pouch, never in the suitcase. Pack your passport, valid at least six months beyond travel, printed copies of your Hajj visa and permit with photos of both saved on your phone as backup, and your Nusuk card. Keep group leader contact details and meeting points saved on paper and phone, because phones die and paper does not. Carry Saudi riyals in cash for small purchases, water and taxis, plus a bank card as backup, and tell your bank before travel so payments are not blocked.

Ihram and clothing: Men pack two or three sets of unstitched Ihram cloths, plus a simple Ihram belt with a pocket for cash and cards. Women pack loose, modest, breathable clothing in plain colours, with no special Ihram garment needed. Read the Ihram rules before packing anything scented. Pack broken in, comfortable sandals that leave the ankle bone uncovered for men in Ihram, and a spare pair if possible. Add extra socks and a light layer for cool nights.

The Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah day bag: This is the heart of the article. Pack it the night before, and check it against each stop.

For Mina (tent stay):

  • The small day bag itself, packed light
  • Extra clothing and a small laundry bag
  • Personal toiletries and a small towel
  • Sun cream and a light umbrella
  • Phone, cable and power bank
  • A reusable water bottle
  • Earplugs and a sleep mask for shared tents

For Arafat (a long hot day outdoors):

  • Water
  • Medication
  • Phone with emergency numbers saved
  • Group meeting point details
  • Tissues and wipes
  • Hand sanitiser
  • Sun cream and umbrella
  • A light layer
  • Snacks such as dates and nuts

For Muzdalifah (an open air night under the sky):

  • A small foldable mat or thin sleeping bag
  • A light blanket or shawl
  • A pouch for the pebbles collected for the stoning at Jamarat
  • Comfortable footwear
  • Medicines needed overnight

Toiletries and health: Once you enter Ihram, scented products are not permitted, so pack unscented soap, shampoo, deodorant and sunscreen, plus unscented wet wipes. Bring basic medication with a copy of each prescription, rehydration salts for hot days, and a small blister kit. Check required vaccinations with your Hajj group well before travel.

What not to pack: Leave valuables beyond the minimum at home. Skip large bedding for Muzdalifah: a thin mat is enough for one night, and bulky bedding is hard to carry. Leave heavy books behind, and pack nothing scented for use during Ihram. The Saudi Ministry of Hajj advises keeping bags light with personal essentials only.

Checklist summary: three bags; documents, Nusuk card and riyals in the body pouch; two or three Ihram sets for men, plain breathable clothing for women; broken in sandals plus a spare pair; a day bag packed for Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah; unscented toiletries; medication with prescriptions; nothing heavy, scented or precious. Also travelling for Umrah? Compare this list with our Umrah packing list. Then pack light, because every extra kilo is carried through heat, crowds and long walks.
