Haramain High-Speed Train — Makkah to Madinah Guide

Almost every Umrah journey has the same long leg in the middle: Makkah to Madinah, some 450 km of desert highway. By coach or car that drive takes around five hours. The Haramain High-Speed Train does it in about two — air-conditioned, seated, and with the desert passing at up to 300 km/h. Opened in 2018 and built for pilgrims above all, it has quietly become the easiest way to travel between the two holy cities. This guide covers the stations, booking, luggage and the small details that matter on the day. Our Umrah for Elderly & Disabled guide already recommends it for older travellers; here is the full picture for everyone.

The five stations:

  • Makkah — in the Rusaiyfah district, a short drive from Masjid al-Haram.
  • Jeddah (Al Sulaymaniyah) — the city's main Haramain station.
  • King Abdulaziz International Airport — a station inside Jeddah's airport, so you can step off a flight and onto the train.
  • King Abdullah Economic City — serving KAEC and Rabigh.
  • Madinah — on the eastern side of the city, a short drive from the Prophet's Mosque.

Journey times: Makkah to Madinah takes about 2 hours on the through services, against roughly 5 hours by road. Makkah to Jeddah airport is under an hour, which makes the train a realistic option straight after landing — or for the return to your flight home.

Booking, in brief:

  • Book on the official Haramain (HHR) website or app, or through Saudi Arabia Railways. You choose your train, class and seat, pay by card, and receive an e-ticket with a QR code — keep it on your phone and carry your passport or ID, as staff check them together.
  • Two classes: Economy and Business, both air-conditioned with allocated seating; Business adds wider seats and lounge access at the main stations. Fares vary with class, season and how early you book — the cheapest tickets go first, above all in Ramadan and the school holidays, so book as soon as your dates are fixed.
  • Arrive early: plan to be at the station at least 30 to 45 minutes before departure. Ticket gates and boarding close shortly before the train leaves, and the stations are large — the walk to the platform takes longer than you think.

Luggage: The standard allowance is one large bag (up to 25 kg) plus a hand bag per passenger; oversized and extra items are restricted, and bags are screened at the station entrance like at an airport. Confirm the current allowance when you book, keep medicines and documents in your hand bag, and label everything — stations at prayer times and peak hours are busy places. The last stretch — station to Haram: Neither terminus is at the mosque door. At both ends you finish the journey by taxi, ride-hailing car or shuttle bus — around 10 to 20 minutes depending on traffic and prayer times. Build that time into your plans, especially if you are heading to a booked Rawdah slot (see our Rawdah Permit guide) or a departing flight. Travellers from Pakistan will find how the train fits a package itinerary in our Umrah from Pakistan guide.

Coming from Jeddah airport for Umrah? Mind the Miqat: If you land at Jeddah and take the train straight to Makkah to begin Umrah, remember that the line carries you across the Miqat boundary quickly, and the train does not stop for Ihram. The common, safe practice is to enter Ihram before you board — many pilgrims change and make their intention at the airport. Our Miqat guide explains the boundary and the rules in full; if your exact case is unusual, ask a qualified scholar before you travel.

Train or road — which should you choose? For most pilgrims the train wins on time, comfort and rest: you arrive in Madinah ready to go to the Prophet's Mosque, not worn out from five hours on a coach. Private cars keep one advantage — door-to-door travel, which can matter with very elderly passengers, lots of luggage or small children (see our Umrah with Kids guide). If you do take the road, the same journey is covered in the travel sections of our Umrah for First-Timers guide. And once you arrive, our Madinah Ziyarat Guide plans your days in the second city.

Quick questions:

  • How long is Makkah to Madinah by Haramain train? About 2 hours on through services, at speeds of up to 300 km/h — against roughly 5 hours by coach or car on the highway.
  • How do I book Haramain train tickets? On the official Haramain (HHR) website or app, or through Saudi Arabia Railways. Pick your train and class (Economy or Business), pay by card, and travel on the e-ticket QR code with your passport or ID. Book early in Ramadan and holiday periods — the cheapest fares sell out first.
  • Are the stations close to the Haram? Not at the door: Makkah station (Rusaiyfah) and Madinah station are each a short taxi, ride-hailing or shuttle ride — roughly 10 to 20 minutes — from the mosque. Arrive at the station 30 to 45 minutes before your train, and allow the same margin at the other end.
