How Long Does Umrah Take? A Realistic Timing Guide

First-time pilgrims often ask how long Umrah takes, and the honest answer is: the rites take hours, not days. Unlike Hajj, which takes five to six days, the four Umrah rites fit into a single session of a few hours. This guide gives a realistic rite-by-rite breakdown so you can plan your day. If you have never been, our first-timers guide explains the whole journey from home to Haram.

The short answer: In normal crowds, most pilgrims finish in 2 to 4 hours from entering the Haram to the haircut. First-timers usually take 3 to 5 hours because they move slowly, stop for duas, and learn the route as they go. In peak times, such as the last ten nights of Ramadan, the same rites can stretch to 5 or 6 hours.

Rite by rite: Entering Ihram: 30 to 60 minutes for bathing, changing into Ihram, two rak'ahs, intention and Talbiyah at the Miqat. Tawaf, seven circuits: 45 to 90 minutes on the ground floor Mataf when the crowd moves steadily; on the upper floors or roof, where the circuit is much wider, it can take 1.5 to 2 hours. Two rak'ahs and Zamzam: 10 to 15 minutes behind Maqam Ibrahim, then Zamzam water. Sa'i, seven lengths: 45 to 90 minutes at walking pace; the full Sa'i distance is about 3 kilometres. Haircut (Halq or Taqsir): 10 to 20 minutes at the barber stations near the Haram. Our guides to the Miqat points, Ihram rules, Tawaf and Sa'i cover each stage in full.

What makes it longer or shorter: Crowds are the biggest factor. A Tawaf that takes 30 minutes off-season can take 2 hours in peak season. Floor choice: the ground floor is the shortest distance but the most crowded; upper floors are longer but calmer. Timing: the Mataf is quietest between 8 and 10 in the morning and between 1 and 3 at night. It is fullest right after Fajr and Maghrib prayers, when prayer crowds merge with Tawaf. Fitness and mobility: elderly pilgrims and wheelchair users should allow extra time; an electric scooter for Tawaf and Sa'i can bring the total under 2 hours, as our elderly and disabled Umrah guide explains. Rest breaks: there is no rule against resting between rites. Sit, drink Zamzam, and continue when ready. Heat slows everything down too, so read our summer heat safety guide before a hot-season trip.

Is there a time limit? No. There is no fixed time limit on completing Umrah once you start. You remain in Ihram until you finish Sa'i and cut your hair, so most pilgrims finish in one continuous session rather than spreading it across days. Take the time you need.

Planning your trip around the timing: Do Umrah on your first day in Makkah, then keep 2 to 3 days in the city so you are not rushed. The rites are a few hours, but the journey deserves unhurried days for prayer at the Haram, rest and Ziyarat. Avoid doing Umrah the moment you land, exhausted from a long flight. Rest first, then enter Ihram fresh. Book your slot ahead with our step-by-step Nusuk Umrah permit guide.

Common first-timer mistakes: Counting fourteen Sa'i lengths instead of seven. One length from Safa to Marwah is one; Marwah back to Safa is two, and seven lengths end at Marwah. Rushing through Tawaf to beat the crowd and missing the duas. Choosing the busiest hour, right after Maghrib, for a first Tawaf.

Frequently asked questions: How long does Umrah take for the first time? About 3 to 5 hours including rest breaks. Can I split Umrah across two days? The rites are normally done in one session, but resting between rites is allowed, and there is no fixed deadline. Does the time include travel to Makkah? No. The hours above cover the rites inside the Haram only. Add your travel time from Jeddah or Madinah separately.
