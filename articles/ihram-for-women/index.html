Ihram for Women — Rules, What to Wear & Common Mistakes

Many sisters prepare for Umrah by asking what their Ihram clothing should look like, often picturing a special white outfit like the men's. In fact, women have no special two-piece Ihram cloth. A woman enters Ihram in her normal modest clothes, and the rules that follow are mostly about two things she must leave off, plus a few habits to adjust. This guide is the women's companion to our general Ihram Rules article, which covers the rules both genders share.

What to wear: Enter Ihram in your ordinary modest dress: a loose abaya and hijab are the usual choice. The colour is yours to choose; black, white and every other colour are all allowed. What matters is the condition of the clothing: it must be loose, not tight, and not transparent, and it should be plain, without adornment meant to attract attention. Ordinary stitched clothes, socks and closed shoes are all fine for a woman in Ihram.

The two things forbidden: The Prophet ﷺ said: "A woman in the state of Ihram must neither wear niqab nor gloves" (Sahih al-Bukhari, narrated from Ibn Umar).

Covering the face without a niqab: The ban is on the niqab, a veil tied or fitted onto the face, and on gloves covering the hands. A woman in Ihram leaves her face and hands uncovered. If non-mahram men pass by, she may still cover her face by letting her khimar or jilbab hang down over her face, untied, so the cloth falls loosely in front of it. This is what Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) described the women doing with the Prophet ﷺ during Hajj, as narrated in Sunan Abi Dawood: when riders passed, they lowered their garments over their faces, and when the men had gone, they uncovered them again.

Other rules for a woman in Ihram:

  • No perfume on the body or on the clothes, including scented soap, creams and deodorant, from the moment she enters Ihram.
  • Ghusl before Ihram is allowed and recommended for cleanliness, even though it is not a condition for the Ihram to count.
  • Menses does not prevent entering Ihram. A woman in her period makes the intention and enters Ihram like everyone else; she simply waits until she is pure before performing Tawaf.
  • She says the Talbiyah quietly, only loud enough to hear herself, not raising her voice like the men.
  • There is no idtiba for women: she keeps her right shoulder covered, and she does not take the brisk walk in the first circuits of Tawaf; she walks at her normal pace throughout.
  • After Sa'i, she ends her Umrah by shortening her hair by a fingertip's length, rather than shaving or cutting it short like the men.

Common mistakes to avoid:

  • Wearing a tied niqab in Ihram, instead of letting a loose cloth hang over the face when men pass.
  • Wearing gloves, even thin ones, while in the state of Ihram.
  • Using perfume or scented products after entering Ihram, including scented wipes and hotel soap.
  • Wearing clothes that are tight or see-through, which break the modesty that Ihram clothing is meant to keep.
  • Uncovering the face deliberately in crowds out of habit or for photographs, when the loose hanging cloth would cover it.

With these few rules kept, a woman's Ihram is simple: her everyday modest dress, an uncovered face and hands, and a quiet Talbiyah.
