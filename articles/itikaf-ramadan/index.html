I'tikaf in Ramadan at Masjid al-Haram and Masjid Nabawi: Registration, Rules and Practical Guide

I'tikaf means staying in a mosque for worship during the last ten nights of Ramadan, in prayer, Qur'an recitation and du'a, cut off from the routines of daily life. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) observed i'tikaf in the last ten days of Ramadan every year until he passed away (Sahih al-Bukhari 2026; Sahih Muslim 1172). The Qur'an mentions the practice in Surah al-Baqarah: "And do not approach them while you are in retreat in the mosques" (2:187). In those nights falls Laylatul Qadr, which the Qur'an says is "better than a thousand months" (97:3). Sitting in i'tikaf in Masjid al-Haram in Makkah or Masjid an-Nabawi in Madinah is a wish for many pilgrims, and in recent years it has become a registered system with limited places. This guide explains when it happens, how to register, who can apply and what to expect.

When i'tikaf happens. I'tikaf in the Two Holy Mosques begins on the 20th of Ramadan and runs through the last ten nights, ending on the morning after the Eid moon is announced. You stay in the mosque day and night for the whole period, leaving only for genuine needs. For Ramadan 2026, which falls in late February to late March, the last ten nights are the peak of the crowds in both Harams, and the odd nights are the busiest of all, because Laylatul Qadr is sought among them.

How to register. Registration is online only, through the official Haramain portal of the General Presidency for the Affairs of the Two Holy Mosques, and through the Nusuk app. For Ramadan 2025, registration opened on 5 March 2025 at 11:00 AM, and places were given on a first come, first served basis. The steps are: create an account on the portal with your full name, passport or iqama number, mobile number and email; select the Itikaf service; choose Makkah or Madinah; fill in your date of birth and gender; upload a clear scan of your passport or national identity card; watch the mandatory awareness video about the rules; and accept the terms to submit. You get a notification when your application is processed. Registration for the next Ramadan opens only a few days before the last ten nights, so check the portal and the Nusuk app from the start of Ramadan and apply the day it opens, because the seats fill fast.

Who can apply. Applicants must be at least 18 years old and in good health, and you must declare any chronic illness or medication on the form. For Makkah, the 2025 rules limited registration to Saudi nationals and residents holding a valid iqama, and the Nusuk app registration asked for an iqama number. For Madinah, the portal also accepts passport details from visitors. The rules can change from year to year, so do not book flights for i'tikaf on the basis of last year's rules. Check the current year's announcement on the official portal before you travel, and if you are an overseas pilgrim, confirm in writing with the portal or your agent whether visitors can register that year.

Rules inside the mosque. I'tikaf itself is free of charge. Once you are accepted, you are given a designated area. In Makkah the designated zones are in the basement levels and specific ground floor extension areas, and in Madinah they are in the western courtyard expansions and designated roof sectors, with separate areas for women. Only one small pillow and one sheet are allowed, and large bags are banned, so pack light and use the lockers provided for small personal items. You must stay in your designated area, keep it clean, keep noise down, and follow the instructions of the mosque staff. Sleeping in the prayer halls outside the designated i'tikaf areas is not allowed, and neither is reserving space for others.

Practical tips for the ten nights. Bring a Qur'an, a prayer mat, a small bag for your pillow and sheet, and a charger for your phone, and keep your permit or confirmation on your phone at all times. Eat a simple iftar and a real suhoor, and drink water through the night, because the Haram is crowded and the nights are long. Rest during the day so you can stand in Taraweeh and Tahajjud at night. If you cannot get a registered i'tikaf place, you can still spend the last ten nights in worship in the Haram, in Taraweeh, Tahajjud, Qur'an and du'a, but that is not the same as registered i'tikaf, and you must follow the normal rules for worshippers. Plan your Umrah for before the 20th of Ramadan if you want to enter Makkah calmly, because entry to Masjid al-Haram in the last ten nights needs a Nusuk Umrah permit and the crowds are at their peak.
