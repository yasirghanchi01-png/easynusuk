Jabal al-Noor & Cave Hira — Visiting Guide

North-east of Masjid al-Haram rises a steep, bare mountain with an unmistakable double-humped summit: Jabal al-Noor, the Mountain of Light. Near its top sits a small cave, Ghar Hira — the Cave of Hira — the place where the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ used to withdraw in solitude, and where the first words of the Qur'an were revealed to him. Climbing it is one of the most loved ziyarat (visits) in Makkah. One thing to be clear about from the start: the climb is not part of Umrah. Your Umrah is complete without it; this is a voluntary visit to a place of history, done for reflection, not a rite.

What happened in the cave: Aisha (RA) narrated the beginning of the revelation: the Prophet ﷺ would go to the cave of Hira and devote himself to worship there for many nights, until the angel came to him in the cave and said "Read!" — bringing the first revealed words of the Qur'an: "Read, in the name of your Lord who created — created man from a clinging clot. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, who taught by the pen" (Qur'an 96:1–4; the full narration is in Sahih al-Bukhari 3).

The mountain and the cave, in numbers:

  • Jabal al-Noor stands about 642 metres high, roughly 5 km from Masjid al-Haram — a short taxi ride from the Haram area.
  • The Cave of Hira is tiny: about 3.7 metres long and 1.6 metres wide, sitting some 270 metres above the foot of the mountain. Only a few people can be inside at once, and at busy times you queue on the rocks outside.
  • The climb is about 1,750 steps. The path is stepped and paved most of the way, but it is steep and there is little shade.

The climb, honestly: A fit walker reaches the top in 30 to 45 minutes; most pilgrims take one to two hours with rests, and there is no shame in taking longer. Go at your own pace, stop often, and carry water — you will want more than you expect. Wear shoes with real grip (see our Umrah Packing List), and keep bags and food tucked away: monkeys live on the mountain and are quick to snatch anything edible in the open. At the summit the whole of Makkah spreads out below you, and through a gap in the rocks beside the cave the Haram can be seen in the distance — the same view the Prophet ﷺ knew, with a very different city beneath it.

When to go: Climb early in the morning, ideally setting off at or just after Fajr, or late in the afternoon — never in the midday heat, which on bare rock is dangerous as well as miserable. The mildest months are November to February; our Best Time to Perform Umrah guide compares the seasons. Nights on the mountain are popular in Ramadan, but the path is steep: go in daylight unless you are with people who know it well.

At the top — keep it simple: The cave is a place to look, reflect and remember what began there. No specific prayer or ritual inside the cave is prescribed, and the visit does not need one: pilgrims commonly pray two rak'ah nearby if there is space, make du'a, and begin the walk down. Treat the place with care — leave no litter, write nothing on the rocks, and be patient with the crowd at the cave mouth. At the foot of the mountain, the Hira Cultural District now houses exhibitions on the story of the revelation, worth an hour if you have it.

Who should think twice: The climb is genuinely demanding. If you are travelling with elderly parents, anyone with heart, knee or breathing problems, or very small children, weigh it carefully — our Umrah for Elderly & Disabled guide covers the gentler options, and there is no obligation here at all: the mountain can be honoured from below. Many families split up, with the younger members climbing while everyone else visits the exhibitions at the base.

Getting there: Taxis and ride-hailing cars from the Haram area take around 15 to 20 minutes depending on traffic; agree the fare or use the app meter, and arrange the return before you start climbing, as cars at the base can be scarce at peak times. If this is your first journey, read our Umrah for First-Timers guide for where a ziyarat day fits around the rites, and our Madinah Ziyarat Guide for the visits waiting in the second city.

Quick questions:

  • Is visiting Cave Hira part of Umrah? No. It is a voluntary ziyarat — a visit to a historic place — not one of the rites of Umrah or Hajj. Your Umrah is complete without it, and no specific prayer inside the cave is prescribed.
  • How long does the climb take? The stepped path is about 1,750 steps. Fit walkers take 30 to 45 minutes; most pilgrims take one to two hours with rests. Start early in the morning, carry water and wear shoes with grip.
  • Can elderly pilgrims climb Jabal al-Noor? For most, honestly, no — the climb is steep, exposed and demanding, and there is no lift or cart. Elderly pilgrims and anyone with mobility, heart or breathing problems are better served by the facilities described in our Elderly & Disabled guide, and lose nothing religiously by skipping the climb.
