Madinah Hotels Near Masjid Nabawi — Where to Stay Near the Prophet's Mosque

Quick answer: Almost every pilgrim hotel in Madinah sits in the Markaziyah — the Central Area that rings Masjid an-Nabawi on all sides. Inside it, distance is money, exactly as in Makkah: front-row hotels under 200 metres from the mosque cost the most and ask almost nothing of your legs; the 200–500 metre band is the sweet spot for most pilgrims; beyond the Central Area you trade a lower price for a 15-minute-plus walk or a shuttle. If your Madinah nights are inside a package, the operator chooses the hotel — so this guide also shows you how to check what you are really being sold. For the Makkah side of the trip, see our Makkah Hotels Near Haram guide.

Why pilgrims pay to be close: The Prophet ﷺ said: "One prayer in my mosque is better than one thousand prayers in any other mosque, except the Sacred Mosque (in Makkah)" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1190; Sahih Muslim 1394). Staying a short walk away makes all five daily prayers in the Prophet's Mosque realistic, even for elderly parents.
Please note — ranges, not quotes: Every price on this page is an approximate nightly room range reported in September to November 2026 listings, never a fixed quote. Rates jump in Ramadan, school holidays and busy November weeks. Always confirm the live price — and the hotel's exact name and district — before you book or pay.

The distance bands around Masjid an-Nabawi:

The main hotel areas, side by side:

  • North side (King Fahd Gate side) — the most practical side for families travelling with women: women's prayer entrances are concentrated on the northern and western sides of the mosque, so a north-side hotel shortens the walk your mother, wife or daughters make five times a day. The Anwar Al Madinah Mövenpick here is described by its operator, Accor, as the nearest hotel to the women's prayer entrance.
  • South side (Bab al-Salam side) — the side men aim for: Bab al-Salam, the entrance used for the Rawdah visit and for greeting the Prophet ﷺ, is on the south-western side, and Jannatul Baqi lies to the east. Older, dense hotel stock, much of it a few minutes from the courtyard.
  • King Fahd Road (west) — major chain hotels roughly 300–800 metres out, typically 20–40% cheaper than the immediate Haram frontage for a similar standard of room.
  • As Salam district (north, beyond the Central Area) — quieter streets with mid-range and budget hotels, 10–20 minutes on foot. A fair trade for younger pilgrims who would rather spend the saving on a longer stay.

Which side suits your group?

  • Families with women: start on the north or north-west of the Central Area, near the women's prayer entrances.
  • Elderly parents or wheelchair users: ignore the side and buy the shortest genuine door-to-gate walk. A front-row room is worth more than any saving — see our Umrah for Elderly & Disabled guide for facilities.
  • Men hoping to visit the Rawdah: the south side, near Bab al-Salam, shortens the Rawdah route — but the permit itself is booked in the Nusuk app; see our Rawdah Permit guide.
  • Budget travellers: outer Markaziyah (200–500 m) keeps the walk easy without the front-row premium; beyond that, confirm the shuttle in writing.

Booking inside a package — the checks that matter: On the Pakistani and most other Umrah visa routes, your Madinah hotel is booked by your operator through Nusuk, so the hotel in your package is a decision made for you. Before you pay, get the exact hotel name (not "a 4-star near the Haram"), search its real walking distance to the mosque courtyard, and check whether your room is quad, triple or double sharing — the same package price can hide very different rooms. Our Umrah Cost from Pakistan breakdown shows what the Madinah nights should cost. A "Haram view" room costs more and is worth it to some pilgrims; a high-floor room facing the minarets is the cheaper way to keep the view.

Getting to your hotel: Madinah's Prince Mohammad bin Abdulaziz Airport (MED) is about 15 km from the Central Area, roughly 20–25 minutes by taxi. Coming from Makkah, the Haramain High-Speed Train reaches Madinah station in about two hours, with buses and taxis from the station to Markaziyah; our Makkah to Madinah transport guide compares train, bus and taxi. Once you are settled, plan your days with our Madinah Ziyarat guide — Masjid Quba and Jannatul Baqi are the visits almost every pilgrim makes.

Frequently asked questions:

  • How close should a Madinah hotel be to Masjid an-Nabawi? Inside the Markaziyah Central Area if you can — under 500 metres means every prayer is an easy walk. Under 200 metres is front row and best for elderly pilgrims. Beyond 1 km, only book if a frequent hotel shuttle is confirmed in writing.
  • Which side of the Prophet's Mosque is best for women? The north and north-west sides of the Central Area, because women's prayer entrances are concentrated on the northern and western sides of the mosque. The Anwar Al Madinah Mövenpick on the north side is marketed as the nearest hotel to the women's prayer entrance.
  • How much does a hotel near Masjid an-Nabawi cost per night? In late-2026 listings, front-row 5-star rooms ran from about SAR 470 to SAR 860 a night; Central Area rooms were about SAR 300–500, rising in November; and simpler hotels at the edge of the centre could be found around SAR 370 in quiet weeks. Ramadan prices are far higher — book months ahead.
