Madinah · Ziyarat Guide

Madinah Ziyarat — Complete Guide

Madinah al-Munawwarah — the Illuminated City — is the city of the Prophet ﷺ and the second holiest city in Islam after Makkah. Pilgrims usually spend a few days here before or after Umrah: praying in the Prophet's Mosque and visiting the places of his life. Madinah lies about 450 km from Makkah, and the Haramain high-speed train covers the journey in about 2 hours 20 minutes. One thing to hold on to from the start: ziyarat is voluntary. None of these visits is a rite of Umrah, and none needs a ticket or a permit — except the Rawdah — so go at the pace your heart and your legs allow, and leave anything you cannot manage without regret.

Masjid an-Nabawi — the Prophet's Mosque: Built by the Prophet ﷺ himself after the Hijrah, it is the heart of every Madinah stay, and the Green Dome rises over his chamber at the mosque's south-eastern corner. Enter with your right foot, pray two rak'ahs of Tahiyyat al-Masjid — the greeting of the mosque — and settle into the rhythm of the place; many pilgrims aim to pray as many of their five daily prayers here as they can.

From the Sunnah: the Prophet ﷺ said: "One prayer in my mosque is better than one thousand prayers in any other mosque, except the Sacred Mosque (in Makkah)" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1190; Sahih Muslim 1394).

Etiquette in the Prophet's Mosque: Keep your voice low — Allah says: "Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet" (Qur'an 49:2) — and keep it low everywhere near his chamber. To offer your salutations, enter through Bab as-Salam (the Gate of Peace), on the western side, and walk to the front of the blessed chamber: send Salam upon the Prophet ﷺ, then move slightly to greet Abu Bakr (RA) and then Umar (RA), who rest beside him. Make your du'a to Allah alone, facing the qiblah; the Salam is a greeting, not a request. Photography and filming inside the mosque are not permitted — put the phone away indoors, and never let a picture disturb another person's prayer.

The Rawdah ash-Shareefah: Between the Prophet's chamber and his minbar lies the Rawdah, the most sought-after place of prayer in Madinah.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Between my house and my minbar is a garden from the gardens of Paradise" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1196; Sahih Muslim 1391).

Entry is by Nusuk permit only, with separate visiting periods for men and women, and slots go quickly. This guide does not repeat the booking steps — our Rawdah Permit guide walks through them one by one. Inside, pray two rak'ahs, make your du'a, and keep the visit calm and brief so others can enter.

Jannat al-Baqi: On the eastern side of the mosque lies the cemetery of Madinah, where thousands of the Companions rest — among them Uthman ibn Affan (RA), the third caliph, and the wives and daughters of the Prophet ﷺ. The gates usually open after Fajr and after Asr; men may enter, while women greet its people and make du'a from outside the railing. Go after Fajr, when it is cool and quiet. Our Jannatul Baqi guide covers the visit in full.

Masjid Quba: A short ride south of the Haram stands the first mosque in Islam, founded by the Prophet ﷺ when he arrived at Quba during the Hijrah, and praised by Allah as "a mosque founded on piety from the first day" (Qur'an 9:108). Perform wudu at your hotel before you go — the reward is recorded for the one who purifies himself at home and then prays there.

From the Sunnah: the Prophet ﷺ said: "Prayer in Masjid Quba is like Umrah" (Jami` at-Tirmidhi 324) — and in Sunan Ibn Majah 1412, two rak'ahs there carry the reward of a complete Umrah for the one who comes in a state of purity. Ibn Umar (RA) also reported that the Prophet ﷺ used to go to Quba every Saturday, walking or riding (Sahih al-Bukhari 1193) — so Saturday is the loveliest day to go, and the busiest. Our Masjid Quba guide has the history and the practical detail.

Mount Uhud: North of the city, the red ridge of Uhud overlooks the field of the battle of 3 AH, where seventy Companions were martyred — among them Hamza (RA), the Prophet's uncle. Visit the martyrs' graves, make du'a for them, and stand a moment on Archers' Hill (Jabal ar-Rumah), where the archers' early descent turned the day — a lesson in obedience that still speaks.

From the Sunnah: looking at Uhud, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Uhud is a mountain that loves us and we love it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4083).

See our Mount Uhud guide for the story of the battle and how to plan the visit.

Masjid al-Qiblatain and the Trench: At Qiblatain, the Mosque of the Two Qiblas, the command came in the middle of a prayer to turn from Jerusalem towards the Kaaba — "So turn your face toward al-Masjid al-Haram" (Qur'an 2:144) — and the congregation turned as one; our Masjid al-Qiblatain article tells it in full. Not far away, the site of the Battle of the Trench (Khandaq, 5 AH) — the trench dug on the advice of Salman al-Farsi (RA) — is marked by the small mosques known as the Seven Mosques; a short stop is enough.

The dates market and the Quba area: Madinah's dates are the gift pilgrims carry home across the world, and the place to compare them is the Central Dates Market (Souq Al Tumor), a short ride from the Prophet's Mosque, with more than 150 varieties on sale — Ajwa above all, then Safawi, Sukkari, Mabroom and Medjoul. Ajwa typically runs about SAR 40–120 per kilo depending on grade and packaging; taste before you buy, check the weight printed on the box, and confirm the price on the day, as prices move with the season. Date shops also cluster in the streets around the Haram and near Quba, handy if you are already out on a ziyarat round — most stalls trade from late morning into the night.

Best times for ziyarat: Start straight after Fajr — Baqi is open, Quba is cool and quiet, and you are back before the heat. Late afternoon suits Uhud and Qiblatain, and the evenings belong to the dates market and the streets around the Haram. Avoid the Friday midday crush around the mosque, and give the Rawdah whatever slot your permit gives you: build the day around it, not the other way round.

Practical tips:

  • Women's entry: women pray in their own sections of the mosque, reached by the designated women's gates — follow the signs and the staff, who manage the flows at prayer times. Rawdah visits for women run in their own periods on the Nusuk permit; follow the time printed on yours.
  • Shoes: numbered shoe racks stand at the entrances — note your rack and shelf number before you go in, or carry a small shoe bag and keep your shoes with you; in the courtyard rush, a lost sandal is a classic Madinah story.
  • Gates: learn two or three gates near your hotel and use them consistently — the mosque is vast, and a familiar gate is your landmark. For the Salam, head to Bab as-Salam on the western side.
  • Getting around: most ziyarat sites are a short taxi ride apart; agree the fare before you set off, or hire one driver for a half-day round of Quba, Qiblatain and Uhud.
  • Pace: 2–3 days covers the main ziyarat without rushing — and leaves the best hours free for prayer in the Prophet's Mosque itself. Our Makkah to Madinah guide helps you plan the journey at either end.

Quick questions:

  • Do I need a permit for Quba, Uhud or Qiblatain? No. The ziyarat sites are open visits with no permit, ticket or prescribed ritual. The only booking in Madinah is the Rawdah, which needs a free Nusuk permit — see our step-by-step Rawdah Permit guide.
  • Can women visit Jannat al-Baqi? Women greet the people of Baqi and make du'a from outside the railing; entry inside the cemetery is for men. Women visit the Rawdah in their own permitted periods and pray in the women's sections of the Prophet's Mosque.
  • How many days do I need in Madinah? 2–3 days covers the Prophet's Mosque, the Rawdah, Baqi, Quba, Uhud, Qiblatain and the Trench at a relaxed pace. Stay longer if you can — many pilgrims say the unhurried days of prayer in Masjid an-Nabawi were the heart of the whole journey.
