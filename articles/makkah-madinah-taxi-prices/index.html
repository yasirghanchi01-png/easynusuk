Makkah to Madinah Taxi Prices 2026: Fares by Car, Booking Tips

The road journey between Makkah and Madinah runs about 435 to 450 km along the Hijrah Highway and takes 4.5 to 5 hours by car, depending on traffic and stops. A private taxi is the door-to-door option: it collects you from your hotel in one city and drops you at your hotel in the other, with your luggage in the boot the whole way. Taxi fares on this route are charged per vehicle, not per person, and the price is fixed and agreed before you travel. The table below gives the one-way ranges found across private transfer providers, checked on 25 November 2026.

Read the ranges honestly. Prices vary by provider and by season, and the same car can be quoted at both ends of its range in different months. The figures above are the going rates across several providers, not a tariff set by any authority. Tolls and parking are often not included in the first quote, so ask whether they are covered before you treat a fare as final. A quote far below the range for that car usually means a smaller car, a shared ride, or extras added later. If a company quotes you per person or in another currency, convert it back to one per vehicle figure in riyals before you compare it with the table.

Booking tips that save money and arguments:

  • Agree the full price on WhatsApp before the day, in writing, including any stops you plan to make on the way. A written agreement ends most disputes before they start.
  • Price any stop in advance: a detour to Badr adds roughly SAR 100 to the fare, and other ziyarat stops should be agreed the same way, not negotiated at the roadside.
  • Book a few days ahead in busy seasons: prices rise during Ramadan and Hajj, and the best cars go first, so a booking made in advance locks both the car and the fare.
  • Pay after the ride where the provider offers it; several companies let you pay the driver on arrival in Madinah, which keeps the risk on their side, not yours.
  • Confirm the exact car model, not just the price: a "7-seater" quote can mean very different vehicles, and five hours on the highway is a long time to spend in the wrong one.

Taxi or train? For a solo traveller, the Haramain high-speed train can be cheaper: economy fares run roughly SAR 49 to 250 per person, and the train is faster. But train fares are per seat, while a taxi fare covers the whole car, so for a family of four or more one fixed taxi fare is usually better value, with no station transfers at either end. Our general Makkah to Madinah transport guide compares the train, bus and taxi side by side, and the Haramain train guide covers stations, booking and luggage rules if you lean that way.

Whichever car you choose, agree the fare, the model and the stops in writing before the day, keep the driver's number saved, and give your Madinah hotel name clearly when you book, since most drivers know the hotels around the Prophet's Mosque well. Leave Makkah with time to spare. The Hijrah road is part of the journey, and a calm, fairly priced ride makes arriving in Madinah feel the way it should.
