Makkah to Madinah — Train, Bus & Taxi Guide

Quick answer: The two holy cities stand about 450 km apart, and there are four honest ways across: the Haramain high-speed train (about 2 hours 20 minutes, base fare SAR 162 Economy), a private car (4–5 hours door-to-door, about SAR 405–830 per vehicle), ride-hailing (about SAR 350–500), and the bus (about SAR 60–170 per person, 5.5–6.5 hours). For most pilgrims the train is the answer — the rest of this page covers when it is not, and the station taxis nobody budgets for. If you are costing the whole trip, our Umrah Cost from Pakistan breakdown shows where this leg sits in the total.

Please note — fares, not quotes: Every price below is a 2026 figure checked in October–November 2026. Train fares move with demand and how early you book; road prices are agreed with the driver before you set off. Confirm the live fare before you pay.

The Haramain train — the default choice: The Haramain High-Speed Railway runs the 449 km line between the two cities via Jeddah at speeds of up to 300 km/h, covering Makkah to Madinah in about 2 hours 20 minutes. The official base fare is SAR 162 in Economy and SAR 455 in Business, one way (sar.hhr.sa, October 2026), before the 15% VAT and fees; in practice, 2026 tickets are typically found around SAR 145–200 in Economy and SAR 240–300 in Business, depending on the train and how early you book. Book at sar.hhr.sa, in the HHR app, in the Nusuk app — the same account our Nusuk App Complete Guide walks you through — or via the call centre on 920 004 433. Our dedicated Haramain Train guide covers the stations, booking and the journey day itself in full.

Luggage, and the station gap:

  • Luggage: one large bag up to 25 kg (65 × 55 × 35 cm) plus one hand item per passenger — pack to it, and the station stops being stressful.
  • The stations are not at the Haram. Makkah station is about 3.5 km from Masjid al-Haram, a taxi of about SAR 15–30; Madinah station is about 9 km from the Prophet's Mosque, a taxi of about SAR 20–40. Add both rides — and the extra half hour at each end — to the ticket price before you compare it with a car.
  • Book early in Ramadan and Hajj. In the peak seasons the best timings sell out completely; in every season, the cheapest fares go first.

The bus — the budget backup: SAPTCO and Darb Al Watan coaches run between the two cities for about SAR 60–170 per person, taking 5.5–6.5 hours with stops on the way. The bus is rarely a first choice, but it is the reliable fallback when the trains have sold out — and for a solo pilgrim travelling light, it is by far the cheapest seat between the two cities.

Private car — best for families and groups: A car with a driver takes you hotel door to hotel door in 4–5 hours at a fixed price agreed before you set off: about SAR 405–450 for a sedan, SAR 430–550 for an SUV, SAR 530–550 for a van, and SAR 830–1,200 for a luxury vehicle. Split four to six ways, a car can cost less per head than train tickets plus station taxis — with no luggage limit and no station transfers. Agree two things before the wheels move: the full fixed price, and your stops — prayer breaks along the way, and the historic stop at Badr if your group wants it. Where you sleep at the far end matters as much as how you get there; our Makkah Hotels Near Haram guide covers the Makkah end of the journey.

Ride-hailing — Uber and Careem: Both apps work in both cities, and a Makkah–Madinah ride typically comes to about SAR 350–500 — door-to-door like a private car, booked in a minute. The catch is surge pricing: at prayer exits, on Fridays and through Ramadan the fare can climb well past that range, and drivers can be scarce for a 450 km trip at busy hours. Check the fare in the app before you commit; if it has surged, a pre-booked private car at a fixed price is usually the calmer deal.

Which option for whom:

  • Solo or a couple, in a hurry: the train — fastest by hours, and you arrive rested enough to go straight to the mosque.
  • Family or group with luggage: a private car — one fixed price, door to door, stops when you need them.
  • Tight budget, or the trains have sold out: the bus — slow, but it gets everyone there for the price of a modest meal.

However you cross, plan the days at each end as carefully as the crossing: our Best Time to Perform Umrah guide maps how crowds and prices move through the year, and if your wider journey starts or ends at the airport, our Jeddah Airport to Makkah guide and Umrah Visa Fees guide cover those legs.

Quick questions:

  • How early should I book the Haramain train? As soon as your dates are fixed. In quiet weeks, a few days ahead is usually enough; in Ramadan, the Hajj season and school holidays, book weeks ahead — popular timings sell out entirely, and the cheapest fares always go first.
  • Is the train station near the Haram? No. Makkah station is about 3.5 km from Masjid al-Haram (taxi about SAR 15–30) and Madinah station about 9 km from the Prophet's Mosque (taxi about SAR 20–40). Budget the taxi and the extra time at both ends, especially with luggage.
  • Can I take a bus at night? Yes — SAPTCO and Darb Al Watan run services through the day and into the night on this route, and many pilgrims ride overnight to save a hotel night. Confirm the exact departure time and arrival terminal when you book, and keep your valuables with you at the stops.
