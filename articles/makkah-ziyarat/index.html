Makkah Ziyarat — Historic Places to Visit in Makkah

Your Umrah happens inside Masjid al-Haram — but the city around it is the stage of the Seerah itself. The mountain where the Prophet ﷺ hid on the Hijrah, the cemetery where Khadijah (RA) rests, the mosque where the jinn heard the Qur'an: all of them stand within a short drive of the Haram, and together they are known as the Makkah ziyarat — the visits. Be clear about their place, as with our Jabal al-Noor guide: ziyarat is voluntary, not part of Umrah. Your Umrah is complete without a single visit; these places are for learning, reflection and love of the history, and no special rites are prescribed at any of them.

The cave that sheltered the Hijrah: When the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr (RA) hid from the Quraysh search parties in the Cave of Thawr, Allah recorded the moment in the Qur'an: "…when they were both in the cave, and he said to his companion: Do not grieve; indeed, Allah is with us" (Qur'an 9:40). They stayed in the cave for three nights before travelling on to Madinah — the journey that begins the Islamic calendar.

Jabal Thawr — the mountain of the Hijrah: About 5 km south of Masjid al-Haram stands Jabal Thawr, with the Cave of Thawr near its summit. The climb is harder than Jabal al-Noor — steeper, rougher and with less of a made path — and takes most climbers one and a half to two and a half hours. Many pilgrims, wisely, look up at the mountain from below and make du'a there instead; the view carries the story just as well. If you do climb, go at first light, carry plenty of water, and read the honest climb advice in our Jabal al-Noor & Cave Hira guide — it applies here twice over.

Jannat al-Mualla — the cemetery of Makkah: A short distance north of the Haram — walkable for most, a few minutes by car — lies Jannat al-Mualla, Makkah's historic cemetery. Here rest Khadijah bint Khuwaylid (RA), the Prophet's ﷺ first wife and the first believer; his grandfather Abdul Muttalib; his uncle Abu Talib; and his infant sons Qasim and Abdullah. Greet the people of the graves as the Prophet ﷺ taught, and make du'a for them and for your own departed. Entry arrangements at the cemetery change from time to time, so follow the signs and the staff at the gate on the day. Its counterpart in the second city is covered in our Jannatul Baqi guide.

Two stops near the Haram:

  • Masjid al-Jinn — a small, working mosque near Jannat al-Mualla, marking the place where a group of jinn listened to the Prophet ﷺ reciting the Qur'an and believed: "Say: It has been revealed to me that a group of the jinn listened and said, 'Indeed, we have heard an amazing Qur'an'" (Qur'an 72:1). Pray two rak'ah if it is open, and move on — it is a short stop.
  • The Prophet's birthplace — in the Suq al-Lail area, a short walk from the Haram, stands the building known as the Prophet's ﷺ birthplace, today housing a library. It is seen from outside only; pause, reflect on where the story began, and do not treat the spot itself as a rite.

The Hajj sites, on a quiet day: A ziyarat drive usually continues east through the plains your Hajj guides describe: Mina, with its sea of white tents and the pillars of the Jamarat; Muzdalifah, the open plain where pilgrims sleep under the sky; and Arafat, with the small white-pillared hill of Jabal al-Rahmah at its heart. Outside the Hajj season a driver can take you through all three in one sweep. During Hajj itself the whole area is restricted to the pilgrims performing it — see our Hajj 2027 guide for the season and its rules.

Masjid Aisha at Taneem: Many tours end at Masjid Aisha in Taneem, about 7.5 km from the Haram — the Miqat point where the Prophet ﷺ sent Aisha (RA) to enter Ihram for her Umrah. Pilgrims staying in Makkah who wish to perform a second Umrah go out to Taneem, enter Ihram there, and return. Our Miqat guide explains the boundary and the rules in full.

How to do the tour well:

  • Hire a car and driver, or join an organised ziyarat tour. The full round of outer sites takes about 3 to 4 hours by car. Agree the route and the price before you set off.
  • Start after Fajr or after Asr. The mountains and plains have almost no shade; our Best Time to Perform Umrah guide shows how much the season changes the heat.
  • Keep every visit simple: look, learn, reflect, and make du'a in your own words. No prayers are prescribed at these places, and nothing should be touched, taken or written on the rocks and walls.
  • Match the day to your group. With elderly parents or small children, take the flat stops — the cemetery, Masjid al-Jinn and the drive through the Hajj sites — and leave both mountain climbs out. Our Madinah Ziyarat Guide plans the matching days in the second city.

Quick questions:

  • Is Makkah ziyarat part of Umrah? No. Ziyarat means visiting the historic places of Makkah — Jabal Thawr, Jannat al-Mualla, Masjid al-Jinn and the rest. It is voluntary and rewarded as learning and reflection, but it is not one of the rites, and your Umrah is complete without it.
  • How long does a Makkah ziyarat tour take? About 3 to 4 hours by car for the standard round of outer sites, without climbing. Add two hours or more for each mountain if you climb Jabal al-Noor or Jabal Thawr. Most pilgrims hire a driver or join an organised tour and start after Fajr.
  • Who is buried in Jannat al-Mualla? Makkah's historic cemetery holds Khadijah (RA), the Prophet's ﷺ first wife; his grandfather Abdul Muttalib; his uncle Abu Talib; and his infant sons Qasim and Abdullah. Visit to greet the people of the graves and make du'a, following the entry arrangements in force at the gate.
