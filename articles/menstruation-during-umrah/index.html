Menstruation During Umrah: What Women Can and Cannot Do

Many sisters worry about one thing above all when they book Umrah: what if my period comes during the trip? The ruling is clear, and it is merciful. Menstruation does not cancel your Umrah and does not stop you from travelling. It only pauses one rite, Tawaf, until you are pure again. This guide explains what you can do, what must wait, and how to plan.

Enter Ihram even while menstruating: If your period starts before you reach the Miqat, still enter Ihram there. Do ghusl, make the intention, and recite the Talbiyah like everyone else. Purity is not a condition for entering Ihram. The proof is Asma bint Umays, the wife of Abu Bakr, who gave birth at Dhul-Hulayfah, the Miqat of Madinah, during the Farewell Hajj. She asked the Prophet, peace be upon him, what to do, and he told her to do ghusl, tie a cloth, and enter Ihram. Menstrual blood carries the same ruling as postnatal blood here. Passing the Miqat without Ihram is not allowed, even while menstruating, so do not wait until you are pure to enter Ihram. Our Ihram for women guide covers the clothing and rules in full.

What must wait: Tawaf: A menstruating woman may not enter the mosque and may not perform any Tawaf until she is pure and has done ghusl. The Prophet, peace be upon him, told his wife Aisha when she menstruated during Umrah: "Do everything that the pilgrims do, but do not circumambulate the House until you become pure." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1650, Sahih Muslim 1211.) The same applies to the Rawdah in Madinah, because it is inside the Prophet's Mosque: a Rawdah visit must wait until purity, so rebook your permit in the Nusuk app for a later slot; see our Rawdah permit for women guide. When you are pure, our Tawaf guide walks through the rite step by step.

What you can still do: Sa'i between Safa and Marwa does not require purity according to the majority of scholars, so it is valid while menstruating; our Sa'i guide explains the rite. Note that some contemporary scholars advise waiting for Sa'i too, because the Sa'i area is now enclosed within the mosque building, so ask a trusted scholar about your situation if you want to follow that view. You can recite the Talbiyah, make dua, read Quran from memory, and do dhikr throughout. You can also visit the markets, rest, and prepare while you wait.

If your period starts after Tawaf: If you have already completed Tawaf and your period begins, go ahead with Sa'i, then cut a little hair and exit Ihram. Your Umrah is complete. Purity is not a condition for Sa'i, so there is nothing to redo.

If your period starts before Tawaf: Stay in Makkah and wait until your bleeding ends, then do ghusl and perform Tawaf. You remain in Ihram while you wait, so the Ihram restrictions still apply: no perfume, no cutting hair or nails, no marital relations. There is no penalty and no fidya for the wait itself. Use the days for rest, dua, and Quran, and keep your group informed so they can plan around you.

If your group must leave before you are pure: This is the hardest situation, and you should not decide it alone. Contact a scholar or mufti before your travel dates if this is a real risk, and speak to your group leader early. Some scholars, in genuine necessity where a woman cannot extend her stay, permit Tawaf in the state of menstruation followed by a sacrifice (dam) as expiation. This is a concession for necessity, not the normal ruling, so take personal guidance from a scholar rather than deciding it yourself.

Plan ahead: Track your cycle when you book, and try to place your Makkah days in the window after your period ends. Some women ask their doctor about medicine to delay their period for the trip: discuss this with your doctor well before you travel, never at the last minute. Pack enough sanitary supplies, because familiar brands may be hard to find, and keep a small bag ready for the wait in Makkah. If you are travelling without a mahram, our guide to women travelling alone for Umrah covers the current rules.

Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari 1650; Sahih Muslim 1211.
