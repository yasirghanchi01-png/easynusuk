Miqat — Where to Enter Ihram (All 5 Miqat Locations & Rules for Air Travellers)

Every road to Makkah crosses an invisible line: the Miqat — the boundary points fixed by the Prophet ﷺ where a pilgrim travelling for Hajj or Umrah must enter Ihram. You may not pass your Miqat heading for the Sacred House without Ihram, so knowing which Miqat is yours — and what to do on a plane — matters before you ever leave home. For what Ihram itself involves once you enter it, read our Ihram Rules guide next.

Who fixed the Miqat? Ibn Abbas (RA) narrated: "The Prophet ﷺ designated Dhul Hulayfah as the Miqat for the people of Madinah, Al-Juhfah for the people of Sham (Syria), Qarn al-Manazil for the people of Najd, and Yalamlam for the people of Yemen. These Miqats are for the people of those places and for everyone who comes through them intending Hajj or Umrah. Whoever lives inside these boundaries enters Ihram from where he is, and the people of Makkah enter Ihram from Makkah itself" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1524; Sahih Muslim 1181).

The five Miqat, one by one:

  • Dhul Hulayfah — the Miqat for the people of Madinah and everyone who passes through Madinah on the way to Makkah. Also called Abyar Ali (Bir Ali), it lies about 9 km from the Prophet's Mosque; its mosque, Masjid Bir Ali, has washing and changing facilities, and almost every Madinah-to-Makkah pilgrim stops there to enter Ihram.
  • Al-Juhfah — the Miqat for pilgrims coming from Syria, Jordan, Lebanon, Palestine, Egypt and North Africa along that route. It stands near the modern town of Rabigh on the Red Sea coast road.
  • Qarn al-Manazil — the Miqat for the people of Najd — the Riyadh region — and for Gulf travellers approaching Makkah on the Taif road. It is located at as-Sayl al-Kabir, northeast of Makkah.
  • Yalamlam — the Miqat for the people of Yemen and for everyone arriving from the south, including many sea and air routes from Asia whose path crosses it before reaching Jeddah.
  • Dhat Irq — the Miqat for the people of Iraq and those coming from beyond it in the northeast (reported in Sunan Abi Dawud 1739).

The simple rule underneath: use the Miqat of your route. If your journey passes one of the five — by road, sea or air — that one is yours, wherever you originally come from. If you live between a Miqat and Makkah, you enter Ihram from your own home. Our Umrah for First-Timers guide shows where this step sits in the full order of the rites.

Rules for air travellers — read this before you fly:

  • Your Miqat is the point your flight path crosses. Most flights into Jeddah cross Yalamlam (or Qarn al-Manazil on some routes) shortly before landing — so your Ihram begins in the air, not at the airport. Jeddah itself lies inside the Miqat line, so waiting until you land is too late if you intend Umrah straight away.
  • Prepare on the ground. Take ghusl before you travel, and either wear the Ihram garments from home or change into them early in the flight — not in a rush in the aisle. What you still need at that point is only the intention and the Talbiyah.
  • Listen for the announcement. Airlines on Umrah routes usually announce that the plane is approaching the Miqat, often about 30 minutes beforehand. When it comes — or a little before, to be safe — make your intention for Umrah and announce the Talbiyah: "Labbayk Allahumma labbayk..." From that moment the rules of Ihram apply to you.
  • Sleeping through it? Set an alarm for the expected crossing time, or ask the crew or a travelling companion to wake you — the crossing into Ihram should be a conscious, deliberate moment.

If you pass the Miqat without entering Ihram: the correct step is to go back to the Miqat (or to any Miqat) and enter Ihram from there. If returning is genuinely not possible, the majority of scholars hold that you enter Ihram where you are and a dam (a sacrificial animal in the Haram) is owed. Do not guess with something like this — if it happens to you, ask a qualified scholar about your exact situation, and keep our Umrah Packing List habit of preparing early so it does not happen at all.

What about the people of Makkah? Makkah's own residents enter Ihram from Makkah itself — from their homes — for Hajj, exactly as the hadith above states. For Umrah, a person already in Makkah goes out to the nearest land outside the Haram boundary (the Hal) — most commonly Masjid Aisha at Tan'im, about 7 km away — following the Prophet's ﷺ instruction to Aisha (RA) (Sahih Muslim 1211). Before any of this, make sure the paperwork side is settled: our Umrah Visa 2026 guide and the Nusuk App Guide cover visas, permits and bookings.

Quick questions:

  • Which Miqat do I use if I fly to Jeddah? The one your flight crosses — for most Jeddah flights that is Yalamlam. Make your intention and say the Talbiyah on the plane before the crossing; the crew normally announce it in advance.
  • Can I wear the Ihram clothes at home and enter Ihram later? Yes. Wearing the garments is not itself Ihram — Ihram begins with the intention and the Talbiyah. Many pilgrims wear the cloth from home and enter Ihram on the plane as it approaches the Miqat.
  • I am visiting Madinah first — where is my Miqat? Dhul Hulayfah (Bir Ali). You enter Ihram there when you leave Madinah for Makkah, not when you first land in Saudi Arabia.
