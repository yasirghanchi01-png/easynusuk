Nusuk App Not Working? Common Problems & Fixes

Nusuk is the Saudi Ministry of Hajj and Umrah's official app for free Umrah permits and Rawdah bookings — so when it refuses to log you in, never sends the verification code, or shows no slots at all, it can feel as if the whole trip is blocked. Most of these problems are well known, and most have a simple fix. Work through the seven problems below; for the app working as it should, see our Nusuk App Guide.

Problem 1: login loop — "this ID is already registered". This usually means your details were registered on a previous visa cycle, or an agent created the account for you on an earlier trip. Do not keep creating new accounts — that only adds to the tangle. Use the app's Recover Account option to reclaim the existing account instead.

Problem 2: the OTP or verification code never arrives. Check your spam folder first. Then check the number itself: enter your mobile in full international format with no leading zero after the country code — for example +447912345678, not a number starting 07912. And the number must match the one given on your visa application; a different number is a common reason the code never comes.

Problem 3: visa sync delay. If your visa was issued less than 24–48 hours ago, the Nusuk system may not have your record yet. A "something went wrong" or "ID not found" message inside this window is usually a sync delay, not a refusal — wait a full day, then try again.

Problem 4: wrong number or name mismatch. Enter your full name exactly as it appears on your passport. Then check which number you are typing: the number starting 48 is the border number, not the visa number. Visa-on-arrival numbers start 88 when issued at a self-service machine, or 98 at the passport desk. Mixing these up is what produces error 10010016.

Problem 5: no Rawdah slots showing. Slots are released in batches — commonly around Friday and roughly 7–10 days ahead — and they can fill within seconds. Fresh seats and cancellations also appear about every 20 minutes, on the hour and at :20 and :40 past it, so keep checking rather than giving up. A standard Rawdah booking is allowed once every 365 days per person, with the Instant Track option near the mosque as the exception. Once booked, confirm your attendance from 48 hours before the appointment, and note that the QR code only appears about 2 hours beforehand. Our Rawdah Permit guide walks through the booking itself.

Problem 6: stuck or double payment. Never pay a failed transaction again immediately. Check your bank SMS first — the first payment often went through even when the app showed an error. If a refund is due, it returns to the same card you paid with.

Problem 7: the app crashes or freezes. Update the app to the latest version, clear its cache, and restart your phone. If it still misbehaves, reinstall it — or use the Nusuk web version in your browser instead.

Where to get help: Start with the in-app Help and Support section. In the Kingdom, the Ministry's Enayah Centres in Makkah and Madinah help pilgrims in person, and the toll-free pilgrim line is 1966.
