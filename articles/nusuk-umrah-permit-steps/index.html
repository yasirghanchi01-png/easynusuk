How to Book Your Umrah Permit in the Nusuk App: Step by Step (2026)

Every pilgrim who enters Masjid al-Haram in Makkah to perform Umrah needs a permit, and the permit is issued inside Nusuk, the official app of the Saudi Ministry of Hajj and Umrah. The permit is completely free. Never pay anyone for a permit: Nusuk issues it at no cost to every registered pilgrim with a valid visa, so anyone charging you for one is running a scam. This guide walks through the whole process in order, from creating your account to the QR code you show at the gate.

Step 1: download the real app. Install Nusuk from the Apple App Store or Google Play, and check that the developer is listed as the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah. Copycat apps with similar names exist, and only the official one can issue a permit that the gate scanners will accept.

Step 2: create your account. Open the app and choose your account type. Pick Visitor if you are an international pilgrim travelling on a visa. Pick Resident if you hold a Saudi Iqama; residents sign in through Nafath, the national single sign-on service. Visitors register with their passport number, full name exactly as printed on the passport, email address, mobile number with the country code, and a password. Type every detail slowly, because the app checks your entry against your visa record and one wrong digit can stop the match.

Step 3: verify and link your visa. Nusuk sends a one-time code (OTP) to confirm your account; enter it to finish registration. Then link your visa number and passport inside the app. If the app cannot find your record, your visa may not have synced yet: allow 24 to 48 hours after the visa is issued before trying again, rather than creating a second account.

Step 4: book the permit. On the home screen, tap Umrah, then Permits. Select yourself and add any companions travelling with you, so the whole group is on one booking. Pick a date, then a time slot. Slots are colour coded, and the green slots have the lightest crowds. Each time card also shows the prayer period it falls in, for example Before Maghrib or After Isha, so you can plan around the prayers.

Step 5: confirm the booking. The app shows a summary sheet titled "Your Blessed Umrah" with your name, date and time window. Read it carefully, agree to the terms, and tap Confirmed Booking. The permit is issued instantly, with a QR code, a reservation number and your time window. There is nothing to pay and nothing to print.

On the day: the QR code. The QR code appears in the app 2 hours before your slot. Save it offline and take a screenshot as a backup, because the connection inside the Haram can be slow. Officials scan the QR code at the Masjid al-Haram entry gates, so arrive inside your time window with the code ready on your screen. You can hold only one active Umrah permit at a time; if your plans change, cancel the existing permit first and then book the new slot.

Common problems. If the OTP never arrives, work through our Nusuk OTP Not Received? guide, which covers number formats, the email option and the old-account login loop. If registration fails, check for typing errors in your passport and visa numbers, digit by digit. And if you have only just received your visa, wait the full 24 to 48 hours for the sync before concluding that something is broken.

One permit does not cover Madinah. The Umrah permit is for Makkah only. Praying in the Rawdah inside the Prophet's Mosque needs its own separate permit, booked in the same app; our Rawdah Instant Access and Waitlist guide explains the scheduled permit, Instant Track and the waitlist. Book both permits from home before you travel, and your first days in the two holy cities will run to plan instead of to luck.
