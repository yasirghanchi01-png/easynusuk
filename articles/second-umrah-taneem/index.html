Second Umrah in One Trip: How to Do Another Umrah from Masjid Aisha (Tan'eem)

You have finished your first Umrah and you still have three or four days left in Makkah. The question almost every pilgrim asks is simple: can I do it again? The short answer is yes. The majority of scholars allow more than one Umrah in a single trip, especially for a traveller who has come from far away and may not return soon. The method is well known, and it starts at a small mosque on the edge of Makkah called Masjid Aisha, at Tan'eem.

Where the practice comes from: During the Farewell Pilgrimage, Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) could not complete her Umrah because of her menses, while the other wives completed Hajj Tamattu with a separate Umrah. She was upset, so the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) told her brother Abdur-Rahman to take her to Tan'im, so she could enter ihram there and perform her Umrah (Sahih al-Bukhari 1215, Sahih Muslim 1211). That is why the mosque at Tan'eem is called Masjid Aisha. The reward is real too. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'Umrah is an expiation for the sins committed between it and the previous Umrah' (agreed upon by al-Bukhari and Muslim).

Why a fresh ihram is needed: Your first Umrah ends when you shave your head or shorten your hair. At that moment the state of ihram is over. You cannot simply start a second Umrah from inside the Haram, because the miqat for anyone already in Makkah is to go outside the sacred boundary, an area called al-Hill. The nearest and easiest place is Masjid Aisha at Tan'eem, about 7 to 8 kilometres from the Grand Mosque, a drive of 15 to 25 minutes. Other places outside the boundary, such as Ji'ranah and Hudaybiyah, are also valid, but Tan'eem is the one most pilgrims use.

Step by step: 1. Book your new Umrah permit first. The Nusuk system allows only one active Umrah permit at a time, so book the new slot before you book a taxi, and keep the permit ready in the Nusuk app. 2. Do ghusl and trim your nails at the hotel, so you are not rushed at Tan'eem. 3. Take a taxi to Masjid Aisha and agree the fare and the waiting arrangement with the driver before you set off. 4. At the mosque, make the intention (niyyah) for Umrah and pray two rak'ahs, then say the talbiyah. 5. Return to the Haram and perform Tawaf, pray two rak'ahs behind Maqam Ibrahim, drink Zamzam, then do Sa'i between Safa and Marwah, and finish by shaving or shortening your hair. The whole excursion usually takes about an hour plus the time of the rites themselves.

For yourself or for someone else: A second Umrah can be for yourself, or on behalf of a deceased loved one, or an elderly or ill person who cannot travel. Scholars including Ibn Baz (may Allah have mercy on him) said there is no harm in this, as long as you leave the sanctuary to enter ihram. Many pilgrims use their extra days in Makkah exactly this way, dedicating one Umrah to a parent who has passed away.

A note of balance: The Prophet's own practice was one Umrah per journey. He performed four Umrahs in his life, each in a different year, and he never repeated Umrah from Tan'eem himself. Scholars who permit repetition still advise balance: do not exhaust yourself or your elderly family with back to back Umrahs in the heat. One calm, focused Umrah is better than three rushed ones. Go at a quiet hour, such as early morning or after midnight, rest between rites, and keep the second Umrah simple.

Practical tips: Keep elderly family rested and hydrated at the hotel if they are not joining you. Carry water and a small snack. If you are travelling with a group, agree the meeting point at the Haram before you leave, because the mosque gets crowded at prayer times. And if your plans change, cancel the old permit slot before it starts and book a fresh one, because a permit time cannot be edited.
