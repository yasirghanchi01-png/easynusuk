Tawaf al-Wada (Farewell Tawaf): Rules, Duas and Common Mistakes

Tawaf al-Wada, the farewell tawaf, is seven circuits around the Kaaba performed as your last act in Makkah before travelling home, once your Hajj or Umrah rites are complete. It is the pilgrim's goodbye to the House of Allah: you circle it one final time, pour out your duas, and then leave the city. It follows the same form as any other tawaf, which our Tawaf guide explains step by step.

The ruling: For Hajj pilgrims, Tawaf al-Wada is obligatory according to the majority of scholars, the Hanafi, Shafii and Hanbali schools; the Maliki school holds that it is sunnah. For Umrah-only visitors, it is not obligatory: it is sunnah and recommended, because the Prophet (peace be upon him) did not perform a farewell tawaf when he left Makkah after Umrat al-Qada. So if you are travelling for Umrah only, do not worry that your Umrah is incomplete without it. It is a beautiful recommended act, not a condition of acceptance.

The evidence: Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "None of you should depart until his last act is circumambulation of the House" (Sahih Muslim 1327). He also reported: "The people were commanded that the last thing they do should be to circumambulate the House, but an exception was made for menstruating women" (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim 1328). These two reports are the basis of the ruling for Hajj, and of the exemption explained next.

Who is exempt: A woman in menstruation or postnatal bleeding does not perform Tawaf al-Wada and owes no expiation for leaving it. If she becomes pure while she is still in Makkah, she should perform it before she leaves. A person so ill that they truly cannot perform it is excused. But if you can manage it with help, or in a wheelchair, then do it: needing assistance does not remove the obligation after Hajj.

If you miss it after Hajj: If the farewell tawaf was forgotten or left out of ignorance, the ruling is to offer a sacrifice, a sheep slaughtered in Makkah and distributed to its poor, and there is no sin on you. If it was deliberately neglected, the sacrifice is still owed, and it is sinful once you have travelled beyond the distance at which prayers are shortened, about 83 to 85 km from Makkah. If you are still near Makkah, return and perform it. In every case your Hajj remains valid. Pilgrims preparing now can plan the farewell tawaf into their last day with our Hajj 2027 guide.

How to perform it: Exactly like any tawaf: seven circuits in a state of wudu, then two rakahs of prayer. There is no fixed dua written for the farewell tawaf. Recite the Tawaf duas you know, ask Allah to accept your Hajj or Umrah, and make it genuinely your last act in Makkah. Do not go back to long shopping trips or sightseeing afterwards; if you end up staying in Makkah much longer, repeat the farewell tawaf before you finally leave. It comes right at the end of your stay, after Sai and the other rites are complete.

Common mistakes: Skipping the farewell tawaf after Hajj, where it is obligatory for most pilgrims. Doing it days before departure and then staying on in Makkah, so it is no longer your last act. Believing it is compulsory after Umrah and worrying needlessly, when it is only recommended. And a menstruating woman attempting it instead of leaving without it, when the Prophet (peace be upon him) himself excused her. Sources: Sahih Muslim 1327 and 1328, Sahih al-Bukhari, Islamweb fatwa on Farewell Tawaaf, IslamQA on whether Tawaf al-Wada is necessary in Umrah, Office of the Mufti of the Federal Territory of Malaysia Al-Kafi #502.
