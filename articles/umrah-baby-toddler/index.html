Umrah with a Baby or Toddler: A Practical Guide for Parents

Yes, you can perform Umrah with a baby or a toddler, and thousands of families do it every year. There is no minimum age for Umrah. A baby can be carried through every rite, and the parent who takes them shares in the reward. The trip needs more planning than a solo Umrah, but with the right timing, a good carrier and a flexible plan, it is very doable. This guide covers the rulings, the paperwork, prams, feeding and packing.

Is a baby's Umrah valid? A baby is not required to enter ihram, and the rites are not imposed on them. If you want your child to perform Umrah, a parent makes the intention on their behalf, and the child's Umrah is valid. If you simply take the baby along without intending Umrah for them, there is no harm in that either, and you complete your own rites as usual. Carrying a child during worship has a basis in the Sunnah: the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) prayed while carrying his granddaughter Umamah (Sahih al-Bukhari 516). For a baby boy, parents usually use small unstitched cloths if he is put in ihram, and many scholars allow a nappy out of need. Ask a scholar about this before you travel, not at the miqat. If a nappy leaks during Tawaf, stop, clean up and ask a scholar how to continue.

Visa and Nusuk permit for the baby. Every traveller needs their own Umrah visa, including infants. There is no infant exemption and no child riding on a parent's papers, so get a separate passport and visa for the baby through your package or agent. For entering Masjid al-Haram, international pilgrims need an Umrah permit in the Nusuk app. Children generally need their own permits from around the age of five, while younger children enter with their parents. Check the Nusuk app for your child's age before you travel, because the rules for children have changed over the years.

Prams, carriers and where you can use them. A soft structured carrier is the most useful thing you can take. It keeps your hands free in the crowd, keeps the baby above the crush rather than at knee height, and the motion of Tawaf often sends infants to sleep. Prams and pushchairs are not allowed on the ground floor of the Mataf, the open area around the Kaaba, because the crowd density makes them unsafe. They are allowed on the upper Mataf floors and in the Sa'i area, but staff can stop them when it is crowded, and the rules are enforced at the doors. What works for most families: a light, folding pushchair for the hotel, the streets and the courtyards, and a carrier for Tawaf, Sa'i and anywhere crowded. In Madinah, the courtyards of Masjid an-Nabawi are broad and pram friendly, though inside the prayer halls you may be asked to park the wheels at busy times. Choose a carrier with proper waist support and break it in at home for two weeks before you fly.

Timing the rites with a little one. Late at night is usually the calmest time for Tawaf with a baby, and the upper floors are longer but less crowded. Do the rites in the cooler hours, early morning or after sunset, and rest between them. Many parents split the trip: one parent does Tawaf while the other stays with the baby, then they swap. Babies under one are often easier than toddlers, because they sleep in a carrier. Toddlers run, and the crowds move fast, so keep a toddler in the carrier or the pushchair in busy areas and agree a meeting point with your family in case you get separated.

Feeding, sleep and heat. Keep your baby's routine as close to home as you can. Feed on take-off and landing, because sucking helps equalise the pressure that makes small ears hurt. In Makkah and Madinah, keep the baby in the shade, dress them in light clothes, and offer water or milk often. Watch for signs of heat and tiredness and stop for a rest in an air conditioned area. Breastfeeding mothers can feed in the women's prayer areas and family areas, and a light cover helps in busy spaces. For the Rawdah visit, book through the Nusuk app and check the age rules there, since children have not always been allowed in at every time.

What to pack for the baby. Pack the cabin bag as if your hold luggage will arrive a day late: nappies for the full journey plus a buffer, two changes of clothes for the baby and one for you, feeds, a portable changing mat, wipes, and any medicine your baby needs. Most airlines let you check a pram and a car seat free on top of your allowance, and many let you keep a folding stroller until the aircraft door. Keep copies of the baby's passport and visa in your phone. A simple first aid kit with infant paracetamol, oral rehydration salts and nappy cream is worth the space.
