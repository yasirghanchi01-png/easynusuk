Umrah Badal: How to Perform Umrah on Behalf of Someone Else

Umrah Badal, also called proxy Umrah, means performing Umrah on behalf of another person who cannot perform it themselves, so that the reward of the Umrah reaches them. Families most often ask about it for a parent who has passed away, or for one who longed to visit the Kaaba but whose health no longer allows the journey. Badal is firmly established in the Sunnah, as long as its conditions are met. Allah says: "Complete the Hajj and the Umrah for Allah" (Quran 2:196), and the scholars explain that a rite owed to Allah can be fulfilled by one person on behalf of another.

Who it is allowed for: The scholars are unanimous that Umrah Badal is permissible for a deceased Muslim, and performing it for a parent who wished for it is among the finest acts of kindness after their death. It is also permissible for a living Muslim who is permanently unable to travel: old age that makes the journey impossible, a chronic illness with no expected cure, or a lasting disability. It is NOT permissible for a healthy person who is able to travel and simply prefers to send someone else in their place. And where the inability is temporary, such as an illness that is expected to pass, the right course is to wait until they recover and perform Umrah themselves.

The evidence: Abu Razin (may Allah be pleased with him) asked about his elderly father, who could neither perform Hajj nor Umrah nor travel, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Perform Hajj on behalf of your father and perform Umrah" (Sunan Abi Dawud 1810, Jami at-Tirmidhi 930, graded sahih). A woman of Khath'am asked about her elderly father, who could not sit firmly on a mount, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) answered "Yes" (Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim). Another woman told the Prophet (peace be upon him) that her mother had vowed to perform Hajj and then died; he told her to perform it for her, explaining that the debt owed to Allah has more right to be paid (Sahih al-Bukhari).

The conditions: First, the performer must be a Muslim of sound mind who has already performed his own Umrah. This comes from the hadith of Shubrumah: when a man told the Prophet (peace be upon him) he was performing Hajj for Shubrumah and had not yet performed his own, the Prophet said: "Perform Hajj for yourself first, then perform Hajj on behalf of Shubrumah" (Sunan Abi Dawud 1811). Second, the intention (niyyah) must clearly name the person the Umrah is for. Third, one Umrah is for one person only; two people cannot be combined in a single Umrah. Fourth, the rites must be physically performed in Makkah by the performer himself; there is no remote or symbolic Umrah. Fifth, there is no chain deputation: the person appointed performs the Umrah himself and cannot pass it on to a third person. Note that some Maliki scholars disliked Badal for the deceased unless there was a bequest, so when in doubt, follow a trusted scholar.

Step by step: 1. Perform your own Umrah first and complete it fully, ending with the haircut. 2. For a Badal Umrah in the same trip, leave the Haram boundary and enter ihram from outside it, most commonly at Masjid Aisha at Tan'eem, making the niyyah for the other person by name: "O Allah, I intend Umrah on behalf of my father." 3. Book the Nusuk Umrah permit in your own name: the permit is for the traveller who enters the Haram, while the intention is for the other person. 4. Perform Tawaf, Sa'i and the haircut exactly as in a normal Umrah, keeping the person in your intention and in your duas throughout.

If you hire someone: Many families pay a student of knowledge or a resident of Makkah to perform a Badal Umrah. This is allowed, but agree only the genuine costs in advance, such as transport and a fair fee, in clear words. Beware of agents selling a "guaranteed" Badal: no one can guarantee how Allah accepts an act of worship. Choose someone known for honesty, and ask them to confirm once the rites are complete.
