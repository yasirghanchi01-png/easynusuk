How Much Does Umrah Cost in 2026? — Complete Budget Breakdown

"How much does Umrah cost?" is the first question every pilgrim asks — and the honest answer is that there is no single price. Two choices decide most of your budget: when you go and how close you sleep to the Haram. Everything else is smaller than those two. This breakdown goes through the journey line by line — visa, flights, hotels, food, transport, permits and the small extras — with the ranges pilgrims are actually paying in 2026, so you can build a budget that holds. If you are planning the trip itself, read it alongside our Umrah for First-Timers guide.

Please note — ranges, not promises: Every figure below is a range checked in November 2026, not a quote. Prices move with the season (Ramadan alone can add roughly 30–50%), your departure city and your operator. The Saudi riyal is pegged at about SAR 3.75 to USD 1, so riyal-to-dollar conversions are stable; conversions into rupees and other currencies move daily. Confirm the live price of every line before you pay.

1 — The visa: The dedicated Umrah visa costs about SAR 400–480 in government fee and mandatory insurance, before your agent's service charge; the tourist eVisa costs about SAR 535 but is multiple-entry for a year. Our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide breaks both down line by line. Pilgrims from Pakistan cannot apply directly and go through a licensed operator — all-in visa costs there run about PKR 48,000–65,000.

2 — Flights: The most variable line of all, set by your departure city, airline and how early you book. It is also why complete packages differ so widely: full Umrah packages from Pakistan generally run PKR 220,000–500,000+ per person depending on season and hotel tier, as our Umrah from Pakistan guide sets out. Wherever you fly from, the same rule holds: fares climb steeply in Ramadan, Rabi al-Awwal and school holidays, and booking early is the single biggest saving most pilgrims ever make.

3 — Hotels, the biggest lever: In Makkah, price follows metres from Masjid al-Haram. Off-peak 2026 ranges per room, per night: on the Haram plaza and in the Clock Tower complex, about SAR 900–2,500+; 300–600 m out (Ajyad, Misfalah), about SAR 350–700; 700 m–1.5 km in the shuttle zone, about SAR 150–350. Madinah runs a little cheaper: plaza-facing 5-star about SAR 800–1,800; Markaziyah, 200–500 m, about SAR 300–600; 600 m and beyond, about SAR 120–280. Two warnings from every honest operator: rates are quoted per room, so a SAR 400 room is SAR 100 per person in quad sharing — sharing is where packages get cheap — and the same room can cost several times more in the last ten nights of Ramadan.

4 — Food: Both cities are ringed with pilgrim restaurants, and a filling meal costs about SAR 15–30. Budget about SAR 45–70 a day eating economically — pilgrim restaurants, bakeries, dates and Zamzam between meals — or SAR 90–130 a day for a standard mix with hotel breakfasts. Many packages include some or all meals; check what yours actually covers before budgeting twice for the same food.

5 — Getting around: Between the two cities, the Haramain high-speed train runs about SAR 150–200 one way in Economy (about SAR 250–300 in Business), booked at sar.hhr.sa — our Haramain Train guide covers booking and stations. A private car for the same journey costs about SAR 400–550 per vehicle, and a taxi from Jeddah airport to a Makkah hotel about SAR 200–350 per vehicle — per vehicle, not per person, so families often find the car cheaper than four train seats. Short rides inside each city run about SAR 20–50; agree the fare before you get in.

6 — The free lines, and one small one: Your Umrah permit and Rawdah permit in the Nusuk app are completely free — anyone charging for the permit itself is charging for their time, not a government fee. And the journey's best-value souvenir: one factory-sealed 5-litre container of Zamzam from the official counters at Jeddah or Madinah airport costs about SAR 12.50, and most airlines carry it free, on top of or within your baggage allowance — confirm your airline's rule before you fly.

What a whole trip adds up to: Worked per-person estimates for a 10-night Umrah split between Makkah and Madinah, flights included, come out at roughly SAR 3,600–4,900 on an economy basis (quad sharing, shuttle-zone hotels), SAR 5,800–7,900 at standard (triple or twin sharing, 4-star walking distance), and SAR 11,600–16,900+ for premium (twin sharing, 5-star facing the Haram). Treat these as planning bands from published 2026 operator benchmarks, not quotes — your own total will land inside or outside them depending on your city, season and room.

Money-saving tips that actually work:

  • Travel off-peak. The quiet months after Hajj season and outside Ramadan and school holidays are dramatically cheaper — our Best Time to Perform Umrah guide maps the seasons, weather and crowds month by month.
  • Book early, especially flights and trains. Haramain fares and airfares both reward the early booker and punish the late one.
  • Share the room. Quad sharing quarters the per-person hotel cost — the biggest single saving after the season itself.
  • Trade metres for money, honestly. A shuttle-zone hotel at SAR 150–350 with a reliable free shuttle beats a SAR 700 room you resent paying for — but price in the walking if you are travelling with elderly parents.
  • Eat where pilgrims eat. The restaurants one street back from the Haram feed you well for SAR 15–30 a meal; the hotel buffet is a treat, not a routine.
  • Pack properly and buy nothing in a panic. Ihram, sandals, medicines and documents bought calmly at home cost a fraction of the same items beside the Haram — the Umrah Packing List is the checklist to work from.

Quick questions:

  • How much does Umrah cost in total in 2026? For a 10-night trip split between Makkah and Madinah with flights included, published 2026 benchmarks put it at roughly SAR 3,600–4,900 per person on an economy basis, SAR 5,800–7,900 at standard, and SAR 11,600–16,900+ for premium Haram-facing hotels. Ramadan adds roughly 30–50%. Your departure city, season and room sharing move the total more than anything else.
  • How much does Umrah cost from Pakistan? Full packages generally run PKR 220,000–500,000+ per person by season and hotel tier. The visa alone, through a licensed operator with mandatory insurance, is typically PKR 48,000–65,000. The SAR–PKR rate moves daily, so confirm the exact figure with your operator before paying.
  • Are the Nusuk permits really free? Yes. The Umrah permit and the Rawdah permit booked in the official Nusuk app cost nothing at all — they are separate from your paid visa. If someone charges you "permit fees", you are paying for their service, not for a government charge.
