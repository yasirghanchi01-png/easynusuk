Umrah During Pregnancy: Rules, Safety and Practical Tips

Thousands of women perform Umrah while pregnant every year. The ruling is reassuring: pregnancy does not stop a woman from performing Umrah, and no school of Islamic law forbids it. What changes is the planning, not the permission. New to Umrah? Start with our first-timers guide.

The Islamic ruling: Umrah is voluntary worship, and Islam never asks a person to risk their health for a voluntary act. All four Sunni schools permit a pregnant woman to perform Umrah. The guiding principle is that harm is to be removed, and the Quran says Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear (Quran 2:286). If a doctor says the journey risks mother or baby, scholars say postponing is right, and the reward of the intention remains.

The best time to travel: the second trimester: Doctors and Umrah operators agree the second trimester, roughly weeks 14 to 27, is the most comfortable window. Morning sickness has usually settled and energy has returned. The first trimester brings nausea and the highest miscarriage risk, so most doctors discourage travel then. The third trimester is physically harder and brings airline restrictions, so late pregnancy Umrah needs a careful talk with your doctor first.

Flying while pregnant: airline rules: Airlines are stricter than most travellers expect. Saudia requires a medical certificate once a pregnancy passes 28 weeks, the certificate must be issued within 10 days of the flight, and Saudia refuses carriage from 35 weeks (32 weeks for twins). Other airlines are similar: a fit-to-fly letter from around 28 to 29 weeks and no boarding between 35 and 37 weeks. Check your airline's exact policy before you book, not after.

Talk to your doctor before you book: Get a full sign-off and carry a copy of your antenatal record. Saudi Arabia requires MenACWY meningococcal vaccination for an Umrah visa, given at least 10 days before arrival. UK public health guidance says pregnant women should receive it according to its licensed indications in pregnancy, but confirm with your own doctor. Check your travel insurance too: most policies exclude pregnancy, childbirth and related complications, so read the fine print.

Tawaf and Sa'i while pregnant: Tawaf and Sa'i are walking rites: the Mataf circuit is roughly 2 kilometres and Sa'i between Safa and Marwah is close to 3 kilometres. Walk slowly, rest between circuits, and use the outer rings or upper floors. A wheelchair Tawaf and Sa'i are fully valid, and many pregnant pilgrims use one for some or all of the rites with no reduction in reward; our elderly and disabled Umrah guide explains booking. Choose off-peak hours, after Isha or late at night, and keep a companion with you.

Heat, crowds and what to skip: Makkah heat is the most underestimated risk for a pregnant pilgrim, and summer temperatures regularly cross 45 degrees. Travel in the cooler months from November to February where possible, keep water with you inside the Haram, and never let the first day's enthusiasm set the pace for the trip. Do not attempt to kiss the Black Stone while pregnant: scholars agree the salute from a distance is enough, and the crush near the stone is a real risk. Our summer heat safety guide has more.

What to pack: Your doctor's fitness note and a copy of your antenatal record; loose, comfortable clothing and good walking shoes; a water bottle, light snacks and oral rehydration sachets; unscented toiletries for Ihram; and a small cushion for resting between rites. See our Ihram for women guide for clothing rules, our packing list for the rest, and our menstruation during Umrah guide if your period arrives mid-trip.

Duas for your baby: Tawaf is a known time for accepted duas. Two Quran duas parents make for their children are Quran 3:38, "My Lord, grant me from Yourself a good offspring," and Quran 25:74, asking for spouses and offspring as a comfort to the eyes.

When to postpone: A high-risk pregnancy, a history of complications, bleeding, or a doctor's advice against travel are reasons to postpone. Postponing a voluntary act to protect a life is not falling short of worship. It is performing it correctly.
