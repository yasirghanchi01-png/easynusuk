Umrah for Elderly Parents — Tips & Facilities

Quick answer: Taking elderly parents for Umrah is one of the most rewarding journeys a family can make, and the Two Holy Mosques are built for it: free wheelchairs around the clock, bookable electric carts, step-free gates, wheelchair lanes for Tawaf and Sa'i, and staff whose whole job is helping older pilgrims. What decides how easy the trip feels is not money — it is planning around your parents' energy: a hotel on a flat, short route, travel in the mild months, and every booking made before you leave home. This guide is written for the sons and daughters doing that planning. For the full list of accessibility services in both mosques, see our Umrah for Elderly & Disabled guide.

Wheelchairs at the Haram — free first: The General Authority for the Care of the Affairs of the Two Holy Mosques lends manual wheelchairs free of charge, 24 hours a day, in Masjid al-Haram. The published collection points are the eastern courtyard at Bab Al Salam (Gate 19) and the western courtyard at the Al Shubaika Bridge. The chair is for use inside the mosque only and is returned to the point afterwards; you may be asked to leave an ID document as a deposit, and a family member does the pushing. Supply is first-come, first-served and runs short at peak times — Ramadan nights, Fridays and the last ten nights — so collect a chair early, not on your way into Tawaf.

Paid options, when free is not enough:

  • Official wheelchair pushers wait at the mosque's cart counters and entrances. Use the official counters only, and agree the price and the scope (Tawaf only, or Tawaf and Sa'i) before you start.
  • Electric carts via the Tanaqol app: Tawaf 50 SAR, Sa'i 50 SAR, the full Umrah 300 SAR — and pilgrims aged 65 and over pay half. Book in the app before you go; prices can change, so confirm in Tanaqol on the day.
  • A private rental for the whole stay: companies rent wheelchairs — including electric chairs with hotel delivery — at roughly SAR 100–120 per day. For a parent who needs a chair outside the mosque too, this is often the calmest option. Confirm the price and delivery before you pay.

Tawaf and Sa'i with limited mobility: A wheelchair Tawaf is valid — the Prophet ﷺ himself performed Tawaf riding (Sahih al-Bukhari 1633), and Allah says: "Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear" (Qur'an 2:286). In practice: the upper floors of the Mataf are calmer than the ground floor and are reached by lift and escalator; Sa'i has a dedicated wheelchair lane in the middle of the Mas'a, away from the fastest walkers; and the quietest hours — after Fajr and late at night — turn a crowded rite into a gentle one. Rest between the rites: sit, drink Zamzam, and only then begin Sa'i.

Hotels — distance beats stars: For an elderly parent, the best hotel is the one with the shortest, flattest walk to a Haram gate, step-free access and a lift that fits a wheelchair. A modest hotel 200 flat metres away is worth more than a grand one up a slope or a shuttle ride away — and shuttle drop-off points can still leave a long walk. Before paying, ask the hotel directly: is the route from the drop-off point to the gate step-free? How big is the lift? Can a wheelchair be stored or charged in the room? Our Makkah Hotels Near Haram guide compares the areas around the mosque.

Getting there, and between the two cities: Book airline wheelchair assistance when you buy the tickets — it is free, and a staff member meets your parent at the aircraft and stays with them through the airport. From Jeddah, the options are set out in our Jeddah Airport to Makkah guide. Between Makkah and Madinah you have a real choice:

  • Haramain high-speed train (about 2 hours 20 minutes): stations are step-free with lifts, trains carry dedicated wheelchair spaces, and staff assistance is available in the station and on board. The railway also gives 50% off for passengers with an official disability ID, and for one companion travelling with them — check the current conditions when you book. You still need a short taxi at each end. See our Haramain Train guide.
  • Private car (about 4–5 hours): slower, but door to door — no platforms, no transfers, stops whenever your parent needs one. For a parent who cannot manage a station, this is often the kinder choice; our Makkah to Madinah guide compares the costs.

Health — the part families skip: See the doctor before booking, not after: fitness to fly, and a plan for blood pressure, diabetes, heart or joint conditions. Carry all medicines in hand luggage, in their original packaging, with the prescription or a doctor's letter — our Umrah Packing List has the full checklist. Keep dose times steady across the time-zone change, and never let a parent "push through" the heat: Makkah is hot even in winter at midday, so plan rites for the cooler hours, keep water and Zamzam going, and build rest into every day. Take travel insurance that covers medical care and repatriation, and check the current vaccination requirements for the Umrah visa before you book — they change, and they are checked.

Permits — do the phone work for them: Every pilgrim needs their own Nusuk account and permits, including your parents. If the app is a struggle for them, sit with them before the trip: install it, register their account, and book the Umrah permit — and the Rawdah permit for Madinah — as early as the slots open, because the good times go first. Screenshot every permit and keep printed copies with their passports as a backup. The steps are all in our Nusuk App Complete Guide. EasyNusuk is an independent guide site — the permits themselves are only ever issued in the official Nusuk app.

When to go: For elderly parents, choose the mild months — roughly November to February — and avoid the crush of Ramadan's last ten nights and the Hajj season unless your parents are unusually strong. Our Best Time to Perform Umrah guide weighs weather, crowds and cost month by month.

What children should arrange in advance — the checklist:

  • Doctor's check-up done, medicines packed, prescription letter written, and travel insurance bought.
  • Airline wheelchair assistance requested on every flight, including connections.
  • Hotel confirmed in writing: step-free route, lift size, distance to the nearest gate.
  • Nusuk accounts set up; Umrah and Rawdah permits booked; screenshots and printouts made.
  • Tanaqol app installed for electric carts; Haramain tickets or a private car booked.
  • A gentle daily rhythm agreed: one big outing a day, rest after Dhuhr, early nights.
  • Documents in one folder your parent carries: passport copy, visa, insurance, hotel address, your phone number.

Quick questions:

  • Is the free wheelchair at the Haram really free? Yes. The General Authority lends manual wheelchairs free, 24/7, at the eastern courtyard (Bab Al Salam, Gate 19) and the western courtyard (Al Shubaika Bridge) in Masjid al-Haram. Take an ID for the deposit, use the chair inside the mosque, and return it to the same point. At peak times they run out — go early, and keep the Tanaqol electric carts (50 SAR for Tawaf) as your paid backup.
  • Can my parent complete Umrah entirely in a wheelchair? Yes. Tawaf can be done in a wheelchair — the Prophet ﷺ performed Tawaf riding (Sahih al-Bukhari 1633) — on the calmer upper floors, and Sa'i has a dedicated wheelchair lane. With the free chairs, the electric carts and a companion to push, every rite can be completed without walking.
  • Train or private car between Makkah and Madinah for an elderly parent? The train is faster (about 2 hours 20 minutes), step-free and has wheelchair spaces and staff help — but it needs a taxi at both ends and a station to cross. A private car takes 4–5 hours yet goes door to door with stops on demand. If your parent tires easily or cannot transfer, the car is usually kinder; if they are steady and time matters, take the train.
