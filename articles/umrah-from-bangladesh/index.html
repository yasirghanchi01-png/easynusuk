Umrah from Bangladesh — Visa, Flights & Costs Guide

Umrah from Bangladesh is a well-travelled route: direct flights leave Dhaka every week, licensed agencies in Dhaka handle the visa, and a full 10 to 14-day trip can be done on a modest budget if you book early. The rules changed in 2026, so this guide uses the current requirements — the 30-day visa entry window, mandatory Nusuk hotel and transport bookings, and the biometric step that now applies to Bangladeshi applicants.

Do Bangladeshis need a visa for Umrah? Yes. Bangladeshi passport holders must obtain an Umrah e-visa through an authorised operator before travelling. There is no visa-free entry and no visa on arrival for Bangladeshi citizens. The visa is issued electronically through the Nusuk and Maqam systems, so there is no need to visit the Saudi embassy in Dhaka for a standard Umrah visa — but you cannot apply for it on your own. It must be processed through a licensed Umrah agency.

Route 1: Umrah visa through a licensed agency (the route almost everyone uses): Choose a Hajj and Umrah agency in Bangladesh that is registered with the Ministry of Religious Affairs. The agency files your visa with a Saudi-licensed Umrah company through the Maqam system, after booking your hotel and transport in Nusuk.

Key rules for the 1448 AH Umrah season:

  • The visa must be used within 30 days of the day it is issued. If you have not entered Saudi Arabia by then, it is cancelled automatically, so apply only when your flight dates are fixed.
  • The permitted stay is printed on your e-visa. Agencies commonly quote a stay of up to 90 days, but always confirm the exact stay shown on your own e-visa before you travel.
  • The 1448 season runs to 7 April 2027. The last day to get a visa is 9 March 2027, the last day to enter Saudi Arabia is 23 March 2027, and every Umrah visitor must leave by 7 April 2027.
  • Since July 2026 there is also a one-year multiple-entry Umrah visa, valid for 365 days with a cumulative stay of up to 90 days. Each entry needs its own service package booked through a Nusuk-approved provider, plus an Umrah permit in the Nusuk app. It cannot be used during the Hajj season.
  • Umrah visas are not issued in the weeks around Hajj.

Route 2: Saudi tourist eVisa (only if you qualify): Bangladesh is not on the list of nationalities eligible for the Saudi tourist eVisa. A Bangladeshi passport holder can still apply if you hold a valid US, UK or Schengen visa that has been used at least once, are a permanent resident of the US, the UK or an EU country, or are a resident of a GCC country. The eVisa is valid for one year with multiple entries, each visit up to 90 days, and it permits Umrah outside the Hajj season. You book your own Umrah permit in the Nusuk app.

Route 3: the 96-hour stopover visa (free): If you are flying with Saudia or flynas and transiting through Saudi Arabia to another country, you can get a free 96-hour stopover visa and perform Umrah within those four days, with a Nusuk permit. It is a useful option if you are already travelling through Jeddah, but it is a short window for a full Umrah trip.

What changed in 2026: Three changes matter most for Bangladeshi pilgrims. First, the visa entry window is now 30 days from issue, not 90 — book your flights first, then apply for the visa. Second, Nusuk-verified hotel and transport bookings are now mandatory: every application must be tied to confirmed accommodation and transport before the visa is filed. Third, biometrics are now mandatory for Bangladeshi applicants: you must enrol your fingerprints and photo in the official Saudi Visa Bio app before the visa is issued.

Documents checklist:

  • Passport valid for at least six months from entry, with two or more blank pages.
  • Recent passport photograph with a white background.
  • Meningococcal (ACWY) vaccination certificate, taken at least 10 days before arrival, for everyone aged one year and over.
  • Fingerprints and photo enrolled in the Saudi Visa Bio app.
  • Hotel and transport confirmed in Nusuk by your agency before the visa is issued, with the booking reference.
  • Confirmed return air ticket — this must be shown when the file is opened.
  • A dual-currency card or cash in US dollars or Saudi riyals for spending in the Kingdom.

Flights from Dhaka to Jeddah and Madinah: Biman Bangladesh Airlines and Saudia operate direct flights from Dhaka (DAC) to Jeddah (JED) and Madinah (MED) in about six to six and a half hours. One-stop budget options include Gulf Air, SalamAir, Jazeera Airways, Air Arabia and Kuwait Airways. If you fly into Jeddah, wear your Ihram before crossing the Miqat — the pilot announces it about 45 minutes before landing on Biman and Saudia flights.

  • One-stop round trip: BDT 56,000 to BDT 68,000.

Full cost breakdown: a 10-day Umrah from Bangladesh: All figures are per person in Bangladeshi taka (BDT), based on 2026 operator and guide prices. Prices move with the season, the hotel's distance from the Haram, and how early you book — always confirm the final price in writing before paying.

Visa and insurance:

  • Official Saudi visa fee: SAR 300, plus mandatory insurance.
  • Agency visa-only price: BDT 15,500 to BDT 19,500, or around BDT 17,000 with hotel and food booking references provided. Without those references, agencies quote around BDT 20,000. For the full fee picture, see our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide.

Flights (return, Dhaka to Jeddah or Madinah):

  • Budget, one-stop: BDT 56,000 to BDT 68,000.
  • Direct, Biman or Saudia: BDT 74,000 to BDT 92,000.

Hotels (5 nights Makkah plus 4 nights Madinah, per person on a sharing basis):

  • Budget three-star with shuttle: from around BDT 22,000.
  • Walking-distance four or five-star: up to around BDT 55,000.

Food and local transport:

  • Food: budget around BDT 1,000 to BDT 1,500 a day at local restaurants if your package does not include meals.
  • Makkah to Madinah: the Haramain high-speed train costs roughly SAR 50 to SAR 150 per person, booked on sar.hhr.sa. See our Makkah–Madinah transport guide for train times, bus options and fares. Airport transfers and ziyarat transport are usually included in a package.

Package or DIY? A package is the simplest choice and is how most Bangladeshis travel, because the visa itself is issued through an agency. But once you hold a valid Umrah e-visa or stopover visa, you are free to book your own flights and hotels and travel at your own pace. If you go DIY, book your Nusuk hotel and transport first, because the visa application depends on them.

Before you fly: the Nusuk app: Once your visa is issued, create an account on the official Nusuk app using your visa number and passport number — our Nusuk app guide walks through setup step by step. Use it to book your Umrah appointment slot and your Rawdah permit in Madinah. Rawdah permits for men and women open on separate schedules, so check Nusuk early and book as soon as your slot window opens. Permits are free and are issued only through the official Nusuk app.

Avoiding scams: Umrah fraud is a real problem, and the protection is simple. Use only an agency registered under the Hajj and Umrah Management Act 2021, ask for its licence number, and find it on the Ministry of Religious Affairs' approved list before paying. Get the package in writing: hotel names and their distance from the Haram, flight details, transport, meals and what is not included. Beware of prices far below the market — they usually mean a hotel far from the Haram or a visa that never arrives.

Hajj is a separate journey: Umrah and Hajj are different. You cannot perform the obligatory Hajj on an Umrah visa, a transit visa or a tourist visa. Hajj from Bangladesh runs on a strict quota system through the Ministry of Religious Affairs portal (hajj.gov.bd), with official pre-registration and final registration using your NID and e-passport. Saudi authorities enforce heavy penalties for attempting Hajj without an official Hajj permit — fines of SAR 10,000 or more, deportation and a 10-year entry ban.

Frequently asked questions:

  • Can Bangladeshis apply for an Umrah visa themselves on Nusuk? Not in practice. Bangladeshi ordinary passport holders get their Umrah visa through a Hajj and Umrah agency licensed by the Ministry of Religious Affairs, which works with a Saudi Umrah company. You still use the Nusuk app yourself for Umrah and Rawdah permits.
  • How much does an Umrah visa cost from Bangladesh? The official Saudi fee is SAR 300 plus mandatory insurance. Agencies in Dhaka charge around BDT 15,500 to BDT 20,000 for the visa and insurance, depending on whether hotel and food booking references are provided. A full 10-day trip usually totals BDT 1,16,000 to BDT 2,15,000 per person, depending on flights and hotels.
  • Can women travel for Umrah from Bangladesh without a mahram? Women aged 18 and over may perform Umrah without a mahram when travelling with an organised group, under the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah's rules. Check the current rule with your agency before booking, and read our guide for women travelling alone for the practical details.
