Umrah from Nigeria — Visa & Flights Guide

If you are searching for how to perform Umrah from Nigeria, the visa is the first thing to get right. Nigeria is not on Saudi Arabia's tourist eVisa list, and there is no visa on arrival on a Nigerian passport — so almost every Nigerian pilgrim travels on a dedicated Umrah visa arranged through a licensed operator, with the visa linked to verified hotel and transport bookings on the Nusuk system. This guide covers the routes that genuinely exist in the 1448 AH season, the documents you need — including the yellow fever and polio rules that apply specifically to travellers from Nigeria — and flights from Lagos, Abuja and Kano.

"And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House — for whoever is able to find thereto a way." (Quran 3:97, Sahih International)

Do Nigerians need a visa for Umrah? Yes. Every Nigerian passport holder needs a valid Saudi visa before travelling. There is no visa-free entry and no visa on arrival for Nigerian citizens travelling on an ordinary Nigerian passport alone. Anyone who promises you a tourist eVisa on a Nigerian passport alone is mistaken — Nigeria is not among the eligible nationalities for that visa.

Route 1: Umrah visa through a licensed operator via Nusuk Masar (the route almost everyone uses): You book through a licensed Umrah operator in Nigeria. The operator works with a Saudi-approved Umrah services company and files your application on the Ministry's operator platform, Nusuk Masar, together with a booked package. Since 2025, every Umrah visa must be linked to verified Nusuk hotel and ground transport bookings — there is no visa without a package attached, which is why honest operators sell packages rather than bare visas. The same operator-based system applies to our neighbouring guides: see Umrah from India and Umrah from Bangladesh for how the route compares.

Route 2: apply directly on the Nusuk Umrah service: Since August 2025, the Nusuk Umrah service also allows pilgrims to apply for the visa and book accommodation, transport and tours directly on the Nusuk platform, rather than only through an agent. Whichever route you use, you still book your Umrah permit and your Rawdah permit yourself in the Nusuk app — our Nusuk app guide walks through the setup step by step.

Key rules for the 1448 AH Umrah season:

  • Under rules in force since early November 2025, a standard Umrah visa must be used for entry within 30 days of issuance, down from 90 days. If you have not entered Saudi Arabia by then, the visa lapses — so fix your flight dates first, then apply.
  • Once you enter, the maximum stay is 90 days.
  • The season's closing dates are fixed: the last day for visa issuance is 9 March 2027, the last day to enter Saudi Arabia is 23 March 2027, and every Umrah visitor must depart by 7 April 2027.
  • Umrah visas are not issued, and Umrah is not performed, during the Hajj season itself.

The one-year multiple-entry Umrah visa (announced 20 July 2026): On 20 July 2026 the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah announced a multiple-entry Umrah visa valid for 365 days from issuance, reported by the Saudi Press Agency and The National. It allows repeat entries with a cumulative stay of up to 90 days across the whole year — the 90 days are shared between trips, not reset on each entry. Every arrival still needs its own Nusuk-approved service package and a fresh Umrah permit booked in the Nusuk app before you travel, and the visa is not active during the Hajj season. The announcement did not settle every detail of which nationalities can obtain it and through which channel, so confirm with your operator before planning a year of trips around it. For the wider fee picture, see our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide.

Route 3: the tourist eVisa exception (only if you qualify): A Nigerian passport holder can still reach the Saudi tourist eVisa through an exception: if you hold a valid UK, US or Schengen visa that you have already used to enter that country or region, or you are a permanent resident of the UK, US or an EU country, or a resident of a GCC country, you may apply for the visitor eVisa regardless of nationality — and Umrah may be performed on it outside the Hajj season, with a Nusuk permit. Our Umrah Visa for UK & US Citizens guide explains the eVisa steps in detail.

Documents checklist:

  • Passport valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date.
  • Recent passport photograph with a white background.
  • Meningococcal ACWY certificate — quadrivalent vaccination is required for all Hajj and Umrah pilgrims, taken at least 10 days before arrival.
  • Yellow fever certificate — Nigeria is a yellow-fever-endemic country, so carry a valid International Certificate of Vaccination showing yellow fever vaccination; expect to present it on arrival, so keep it in your hand luggage, not your checked bag.
  • Polio: Saudi health rules also list arrivals from Nigeria for polio vaccination proof (OPV/IPV), so carry your polio vaccination record as well and check the current requirement with your operator before you fly.
  • Your Nusuk-linked hotel and transport bookings, arranged by your operator (or booked by you on the direct Nusuk route), plus a confirmed return ticket.

Visas have reopened — and NAHCON is your regulator: Nigerian Umrah visas reopened in August 2026 after about two years of restrictions, so demand through licensed operators is high and rules are still settling. The National Hajj Commission of Nigeria (NAHCON) is the Nigerian regulator for Hajj and Umrah travel: check NAHCON's announcements for last-minute changes before you pay a balance or fix non-refundable dates, and use only operators you can verify.

Flights from Nigeria to Jeddah and Madinah: Jeddah (King Abdulaziz International) is the usual arrival airport for Makkah; Madinah is the other gateway. Most Lagos and Abuja itineraries connect once — through Addis Ababa, Cairo, Istanbul or Doha — while Kano has seasonal direct services in peak periods. Airlines selling these routes include Saudia, Ethiopian Airlines, Turkish Airlines, EgyptAir and Qatar Airways. Frequently asked questions:

  • Can Nigerians get a Saudi tourist eVisa or visa on arrival? Not on a Nigerian passport alone — Nigeria is not on the tourist eVisa list and there is no visa on arrival. The exception is for holders of a used UK, US or Schengen visa, permanent residents of the UK, US or EU, and GCC residents.
  • How long can I stay on an Umrah visa? Up to 90 days after entry on the standard visa, and you must enter within 30 days of the visa being issued. On the one-year multiple-entry visa announced in July 2026, the 90 days are a cumulative total shared across all trips in the year.
  • Which vaccinations do I need from Nigeria? Meningococcal ACWY for every pilgrim, a yellow fever certificate because Nigeria is endemic, and polio vaccination proof, which Saudi rules list for arrivals from Nigeria. Confirm the current list with your operator and NAHCON before travelling.
  • When does the Umrah season end in 1448 AH? Visa issuance ends 9 March 2027, the last entry is 23 March 2027, and all Umrah visitors must depart by 7 April 2027.
