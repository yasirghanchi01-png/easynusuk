Umrah from Pakistan — Visa & Flights Guide

Pakistan sends more Umrah pilgrims than almost any other country, yet the Pakistani route has one rule that catches people out: you cannot apply for an Umrah visa yourself. The visa is issued only as part of a package, through a licensed Pakistani operator working on the Saudi Maqam system, with your hotels and transport booked first. This guide explains the visa routes that genuinely exist for Pakistani passport holders, the 1448 AH season dates, direct flights from Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, and what a 10-day trip really costs in rupees.

"And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House — for whoever is able to find thereto a way." (Quran 3:97, Sahih International)

Do Pakistanis need a visa for Umrah? Yes. Every Pakistani passport holder needs a valid Saudi visa before travelling. There is no visa-on-arrival for Pakistani citizens, and there is no way to file an Umrah visa application yourself on Nusuk or any other website. Anyone who offers you a "direct" or "visa-only" Umrah visa outside the operator system is not following the rules — see the scam warning below.

Route 1: Umrah visa through a licensed operator (the route almost everyone uses): You book through an Umrah tour operator in Pakistan that is attested by the Ministry of Religious Affairs (MoRA) and works with a Saudi-approved service company. The operator files your visa electronically through the Saudi Maqam platform, with the e-visa issued through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs system behind it — there is no sticker in your passport and no embassy visit for a standard Umrah visa. Since 10 June 2025, a confirmed hotel booking made through Nusuk Masar, with a Booking Reference Number (BRN), plus confirmed ground transport, must exist before the visa can be issued. That is why honest operators sell packages, not bare visas. Our Umrah from Pakistan — Complete Guide covers the operator system in more depth.

Key rules for the 1448 AH Umrah season:

  • The 1448 season opened with visa issuance from 31 May 2026 and entry from 1 June 2026.
  • The visa is valid for 30 days from the date it is issued. You must enter Saudi Arabia within that window; once you enter, you can stay up to 90 days. Apply only when your flight dates are fixed.
  • The 1448 season runs to 7 April 2027. Visa issuance ends on 9 March 2027 and the last entry date is 23 March 2027, and every Umrah visitor must leave by 7 April 2027.
  • Since July 2026 there is also a one-year multiple-entry Umrah visa, valid for 365 days with a cumulative stay of up to 90 days. Each entry needs its own service package booked through an approved Nusuk provider, plus an Umrah permit in the Nusuk app. It is not valid during the Hajj season.
  • Umrah visas are not issued in the weeks around Hajj.

Route 2: Saudi tourist eVisa (only if you qualify): Pakistan is not on Saudi Arabia's tourist eVisa nationality list, so an ordinary Pakistani passport alone does not qualify. You can apply for the tourist eVisa on visa.visitsaudi.com only if one of these applies:

  • You hold a valid US, UK or Schengen visit visa that you have already used to enter that country.
  • You are a permanent resident of the US, the UK or an EU country.
  • You are a resident of a Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) country.

The tourist eVisa costs about SAR 535 including insurance (around PKR 39,600) and permits Umrah, but not Hajj. Our Umrah Visa for UK & US Citizens guide walks through the eVisa steps, and our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide compares both routes side by side.

Route 3: the 96-hour stopover visa (free): If you fly Saudia or flynas and transit through Saudi Arabia, you can take the free 96-hour stopover visa and perform Umrah within that window, with an Umrah permit booked in the Nusuk app. It suits a short stop on the way somewhere else — not a full, unhurried Umrah trip.

Biometrics — the Saudi Visa Bio app: Your operator may ask you to enrol your fingerprints and photo in the official Saudi Visa Bio app. Do it as soon as you are asked: the visa file cannot move forward until the biometrics are done, and leaving it to the last week is one of the most common causes of delayed departures from Pakistan.

Documents checklist:

  • Passport valid for at least six months from your date of entry, with blank pages.
  • Recent passport-size photograph.
  • Polio (OPV) vaccination — required for Pakistani travellers. Keep the record, and get your NADRA digital vaccination certificate through the NIMS portal (PKR 100); carry a printout as well.
  • Meningococcal ACWY certificate: Saudi Arabia suspended the mandatory requirement in February 2025, but many operators still list it — check the current rule with your operator and carry the certificate if you have it.
  • Confirmed Nusuk hotel and transport bookings (with the BRN) made by your operator — mandatory before the visa is issued.
  • Confirmed return ticket.
  • Biometrics completed in the Saudi Visa Bio app, if your operator asks for it.

Flights from Pakistan to Jeddah and Madinah: Direct flights run from Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad to Jeddah (JED) and to Madinah (MED) on PIA, Saudia, flynas, Airblue and SereneAir, with schedules that expand in the peak Umrah months. Karachi to Jeddah is about 4 hours 30 minutes nonstop; Lahore and Islamabad flights run roughly 30 to 60 minutes longer. Flying into Madinah and home from Jeddah (or the reverse) saves one long road transfer — our Jeddah Airport to Makkah guide covers the arrival side in detail.

Scam warning — check the licence before you pay: Umrah fraud rises every season, and the pattern is always the same: a very cheap "package", a request for full payment in cash, and then silence. Protect yourself:

  • Book only with an operator on the Ministry of Religious Affairs verified operators list at mora.gov.pk — check the name yourself before paying anything; do not rely on a screenshot the agent sends you.
  • Under the Hajj and Umrah Act 2024 (rules in force since July 2026), only verified operators may sell Umrah packages in Pakistan.
  • Pay through a bank and keep every receipt. A bank trail is your proof if anything goes wrong.
  • Beware the too-cheap package: a full package advertised far below PKR 220,000 usually leaves out the flight — the most expensive line of all.
  • Get the package in writing before you pay: visa, hotel names and their distance from the Haram, transport, meals, and what is not included.

Frequently asked questions:

  • Can I apply for an Umrah visa myself from Pakistan? No. On a Pakistani passport the Umrah visa is issued only inside a package from a Ministry of Religious Affairs attested operator, with Nusuk hotel and transport bookings made first. The only self-service route is the tourist eVisa, and only if you hold a valid, already-used US, UK or Schengen visa, or qualifying US, UK, EU or GCC residence.
  • Is there a visa on arrival for Pakistanis in Saudi Arabia? No. Pakistani citizens have no visa-on-arrival and no visa-free entry for Umrah. Your visa must be issued before you fly — either the Umrah e-visa through your operator, a qualifying tourist eVisa, or the 96-hour stopover visa booked with a Saudia or flynas ticket.
  • How much does a 10-day Umrah from Pakistan cost in 2026? About PKR 275,000–360,000 per person for an economy 10-day package with flights (quad sharing, 3-star hotels), based on November 2026 ranges. Budget trips start near PKR 245,000 and 5-star Haram-side trips pass PKR 420,000. Always compare itemised written quotes from at least three verified operators.
