Umrah from Serbia — Visa & Flights Guide

Serbian pilgrims now have one of the newest Umrah routes in Europe: Serbia is on the Saudi tourist eVisa list — added on 5 November 2026 — so you can apply for a tourist eVisa online before you fly, get a visa on arrival, or still use the dedicated Umrah visa route when you prefer a package. There are no direct flights from Belgrade to Jeddah, so every route is one stop — the smoothest runs via Istanbul with Turkish Airlines, and the whole journey takes only about 8–9 hours. This guide covers both visa routes, the 1448H season dates, the documents and health rules, and the flights and fares. Travelling from elsewhere? Our newest companion guide, Umrah from Mexico, covers the same brand-new eVisa route for travellers from Mexico, and our Umrah from Hungary and Umrah from Romania guides cover the region for travellers from Hungary and Romania.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Umrah to Umrah expiates what is between them." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1773, Sahih Muslim 1349)

Do Serbian pilgrims need a visa for Umrah? Yes — but you can get yours online in advance or at the airport. Serbia was added to the Saudi tourist eVisa list on 5 November 2026 and is one of the 72 countries named in the official eVisa Terms and Conditions at visa.visitsaudi.com, as also reported by Arab News, Khaleej Times and Saudi Life Guide. The dedicated Umrah visa through Nusuk remains available when you prefer everything arranged in a package. Our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide sets out how the fees compare across the visa types.

Route 1: the tourist eVisa (or visa on arrival): Apply online at the official portal visa.visitsaudi.com. The eVisa costs SAR 535 in total, including the mandatory medical insurance (about US$143; the exact fee is shown in SAR at checkout on the portal). It is valid for 1 year with multiple entries and allows up to 90 days per visit. Your passport must be valid for at least six months from entry. Umrah is allowed on it outside the Hajj season — book the Umrah permit on the Nusuk app — and it is not valid for Hajj, which needs a separate Hajj visa, or for work. A visa on arrival is available on the same terms for Serbian passport holders at Saudi international airports.

Route 2: the Umrah visa via Nusuk or an agent: Apply through the Nusuk app or a licensed travel agent, and enter Saudi Arabia within 30 days of the visa being issued. Since 20 July 2026 there is also a multiple-entry Umrah visa: valid for 365 days with up to 90 days of cumulative stay — each visit needs its own Nusuk service package and Umrah permit — and it is not usable during the Hajj season. Our Nusuk app guide walks through the setup and the permit booking step by step.

The 1448H season dates:

  • Umrah visas for the 1448H season have been issued since 31 May 2026.
  • Umrah visa issuance ends 9 March 2027.
  • The last entry date is 23 March 2027.
  • All Umrah visa holders must depart by 7 April 2027.

Documents and health checklist:

  • Passport valid for at least six months at entry.
  • Meningococcal ACWY vaccination is mandatory, at least 10 days before arrival — the conjugate vaccine is valid for 5 years and the polysaccharide vaccine for 3 years — with the certificate ready to show.
  • Polio vaccination is not compulsory for travellers from Serbia.
  • A yellow fever certificate is needed only if you are arriving from, or transiting, a yellow-fever risk country.
  • Women of all ages may travel for Umrah without a mahram — our Umrah for women travelling alone guide has the rules and practical detail.

Flights from Serbia to Jeddah: There are no direct flights from Belgrade (BEG) to Jeddah (JED). The best route is Turkish Airlines via Istanbul: Belgrade–Istanbul takes about 1h 50m and Istanbul–Jeddah about 3h 45m, for a total journey of about 8–9 hours including the layover. Turkish Airlines Jeddah–Belgrade return fares start from SAR 3,061 (December 2026 dates, turkishairlines.com), and the cheapest round-trip found was about US$414 with Air Serbia via Trip.com (2 stops, 11h 25m, 30 Nov–8 Dec 2026). One-stop alternatives run via Istanbul with Pegasus Airlines (about 8h 35m–11h 50m), Doha (Qatar Airways) or Dubai (flydubai). Fly into Jeddah (JED) for Makkah; Madinah (MED) is the other gateway. Fares are indicative, checked 23 Nov 2026, and change daily — check live prices before booking. Serbia uses the Serbian dinar (RSD), and cards are widely accepted in Saudi Arabia. Belgrade is 2 hours behind Jeddah in winter (CET, UTC+1, against AST, UTC+3) and 1 hour behind in summer, while Jeddah stays on AST all year — once you land, set your prayer alarms by the local Makkah and Madinah times in this site's Namaz section. On arrival, our Jeddah Airport to Makkah guide covers the onward taxi, bus and train options, and if your stay splits between the two holy cities, the Makkah to Madinah transport guide compares the Haramain train, bus and taxi, while our Madinah hotels guide helps you choose where to stay near the Prophet's Mosque.

What it costs: On the eVisa route you pay the SAR 535 visa fee yourself and book flights and hotels separately; on the Umrah visa route the visa comes inside the package. Flights are the biggest variable, then hotels near the Haram. Step by step:

  1. Choose your dates inside the 1448H season.
  2. Apply for the tourist eVisa first at visa.visitsaudi.com — or choose the Umrah visa via Nusuk or a licensed travel agent.
  3. Get the meningococcal ACWY vaccine at least 10 days before travel.
  4. Book your flights 6–8 weeks ahead, and book your hotels.
  5. Book the free Umrah permit on the Nusuk app before entering Masjid al-Haram.
  6. Book the Rawdah permit too if you will visit Madinah.
  7. Pack using our Umrah packing list, with your Ihram garments on top of the bag.

A short practical checklist: Apply for the eVisa first, as soon as your dates are set, and carry a printed copy of the approved eVisa with your passport, valid for at least six months; install the Nusuk app and book your Umrah permit before you fly, and book the Rawdah permit in the same app as soon as your Madinah dates are fixed, because slots go quickly; book flights 6–8 weeks ahead for the best fares via Istanbul; choose a hotel near the Haram in Makkah so the five daily prayers are an easy walk — our Makkah hotels guide compares the areas; and carry your Ihram in your hand luggage and work through our Umrah packing list so nothing essential is left behind — read our Ihram rules guide so you are ready to enter Ihram before the miqat announcement on board.

Preparing for the journey itself: With the visa and flights settled, the rest is preparation. Frequently asked questions:

  • Is Serbia on the Saudi tourist eVisa list? Yes — Serbia was added on 5 November 2026 and is one of the 72 countries named in the official eVisa Terms and Conditions at visa.visitsaudi.com (checked 23 November 2026). Serbian citizens apply online at visa.visitsaudi.com.
  • Can I perform Umrah on the tourist eVisa? Yes, outside the Hajj season, with the Umrah permit booked on Nusuk. The eVisa costs SAR 535 including mandatory medical insurance (about US$143), lasts 1 year with multiple entries, and allows up to 90 days per visit; a visa on arrival is available on the same terms for Serbian passport holders. It is not valid for Hajj — Hajj needs a separate Hajj visa — or for work.
  • Are there direct flights from Serbia to Jeddah? No — there is no direct Belgrade to Jeddah flight. The best route is Turkish Airlines via Istanbul: about 1h 50m to Istanbul and about 3h 45m on to Jeddah, about 8–9 hours in total, with return fares from SAR 3,061. Alternatives run via Istanbul with Pegasus, via Doha with Qatar Airways or via Dubai with flydubai.
  • When does the Umrah season end in 1448H? Visa issuance ends 9 March 2027, the last entry is 23 March 2027, and all Umrah visa holders must depart by 7 April 2027.
