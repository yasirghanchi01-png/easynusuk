Umrah from South Africa — Visa & Flights Guide

South African pilgrims have one of the easiest visa routes in Africa: South Africa is on the Saudi tourist eVisa list — one of only three African countries on it, alongside Mauritius and Seychelles, as listed by Wego from the official Visit Saudi portal (updated 12 Nov 2026). That means you can apply for a tourist eVisa online, get a visa on arrival, or still use the dedicated Umrah visa route when you prefer a package. This guide covers both routes, the 1448H season dates, the documents and health rules, and Johannesburg flights and fares. The same eVisa route is covered for the Southern Hemisphere in our newest companion guide, Umrah from Australia, and for Asia in Umrah from Malaysia and Umrah from Turkey, while our Umrah Visa for UK & US Citizens guide walks through the same online application step by step.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Umrah to Umrah expiates what is between them." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1773, Sahih Muslim 1349)

Do South Africans need a visa for Umrah? Yes — but unlike most African pilgrims, you can get yours online in advance or at the airport. South Africa is on the Saudi tourist eVisa list, and the dedicated Umrah visa through Nusuk remains available when you prefer everything arranged in a package. Our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide sets out how the fees compare across the visa types.

Route 1: the tourist eVisa (or visa on arrival): Apply online at visa.visitsaudi.com. The eVisa costs SAR 535 in total, including the mandatory health insurance, is valid for 1 year with multiple entries, and allows up to 90 days per visit (90 days total per year). Umrah is allowed on it outside the Hajj season. If you prefer, a visa on arrival is available on the same terms at Saudi airports.

Route 2: the Umrah visa via Nusuk or an agent: Apply on the Nusuk Umrah platform — direct application has been possible since August 2025 — or through a licensed South African Umrah agent. Since July 2026 there is also a multiple-entry Umrah visa: valid for 365 days with a cumulative stay of up to 90 days, a service package is required for each visit, and the visa is inactive during the Hajj season. Our Nusuk app guide walks through the setup and the permit booking step by step.

A route that does not exist — the Package eVisa: The Package eVisa is not available for South Africa. It covers only seven countries — Jordan, Egypt, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Mexico and Pakistan — so do not plan around it.

The 1448H season dates:

  • Umrah visa issuance ends 9 March 2027.
  • The last entry date is 23 March 2027.
  • Pilgrims must depart by 7 April 2027.

Documents and health checklist:

  • Passport valid for at least six months at entry.
  • ACWY meningococcal vaccination is mandatory, at least 10 days before travel, with the certificate ready to show.
  • Polio vaccination is not compulsory for travellers from South Africa.
  • A yellow fever certificate is needed only if you are arriving from, or transiting, a yellow-fever risk country.
  • Women of all ages may travel for Umrah without a mahram.

Flights from Johannesburg to Jeddah: Saudia has flown Johannesburg (JNB) to Jeddah (JED) non-stop — about 7 hours over roughly 5,445 km — but non-stop availability varies by season and day, and recent November 2026 searches show no non-stop on many dates. Most current options are 1-stop: via Doha with Qatar Airways, via Dubai with Emirates, via Abu Dhabi with Etihad (fastest about 12h 25m), or via Addis Ababa with Ethiopian Airlines (about 11h 10m). The shortest total journey is about 11–13 hours. From Cape Town and Durban, connect via Johannesburg or the Gulf hubs. One-way fares start from about R4,439 (Travelstart, observed in the past 7 days); returns start from about R12,000, typically R12,000–R16,000. All fares checked 19 Nov 2026, prices change daily. Jeddah is 1 hour ahead of Johannesburg (SAST UTC+2, AST UTC+3). On arrival, our Jeddah Airport to Makkah guide covers the onward taxi, bus and train options, and if your stay splits between the two holy cities, the Makkah to Madinah transport guide compares the Haramain train, bus and taxi, while our Madinah hotels guide helps you choose where to stay near the Prophet's Mosque.

What it costs: On the eVisa route you pay the SAR 535 visa fee yourself and book flights and hotels separately; on the Umrah visa route the visa comes inside the package. Flights are the biggest variable, then hotels near the Haram. See our general Umrah cost guide and the Makkah hotels guide before you book.

Step by step:

  1. Choose your dates inside the 1448H season.
  2. Pick the visa route — tourist eVisa online or on arrival, or the Umrah visa via Nusuk or a licensed agent.
  3. Get the ACWY vaccine at least 10 days before travel.
  4. Book your flights and hotels (or your service package on the Umrah visa route).
  5. Get your Umrah permit in the Nusuk app.
  6. Book the Rawdah permit too if you will visit Madinah.
  7. Pack using our Umrah packing list.

Preparing for the journey itself: With the visa and flights settled, the rest is preparation. Learn the supplications from Umrah Duas and Tawaf Duas before you fly, so the rites themselves feel familiar when you reach the Haram.

Frequently asked questions:

  • Is South Africa on the Saudi tourist eVisa list? Yes — it is one of only three African countries on the list, together with Mauritius and Seychelles, according to the official Visit Saudi portal as listed by Wego (updated 12 Nov 2026).
  • Can I perform Umrah on the tourist eVisa? Yes, outside the Hajj season. The eVisa costs SAR 535 including mandatory health insurance, lasts 1 year with multiple entries, and allows up to 90 days per visit (90 days total per year); a visa on arrival is available on the same terms.
  • Is the Package eVisa available for South Africa? No. The Package eVisa covers only Jordan, Egypt, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Mexico and Pakistan.
  • When does the Umrah season end in 1448H? Visa issuance ends 9 March 2027, the last entry is 23 March 2027, and pilgrims must depart by 7 April 2027.
