Umrah from Turkey — Visa & Flights Guide

Of all the country guides on this site, Turkey's is the most straightforward — because Turkey is on Saudi Arabia's tourist eVisa list. After the expansion of 5 November 2026, the list covers 72 countries, with Turkey listed in the Asia group (Travelobiz; House of Saud; Wego Blog). That means a Turkish passport holder can apply for a tourist eVisa online in advance, or obtain one on arrival at a Saudi international airport, and perform Umrah on it outside the Hajj season. A dedicated Umrah visa through the Nusuk platform remains the other route, including the one-year multiple-entry visa introduced in July 2026. This guide covers both routes, the documents and vaccinations you need — including the chipped-passport rule that catches some Turkish travellers out — and Istanbul–Jeddah flights and fares. Compare the eVisa route here with our Umrah Visa for UK & US Citizens guide, and contrast it with the agent-based routes in Umrah from Egypt, Umrah from Nigeria and Umrah from Indonesia, where no eVisa exists on those passports at all.

"And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House — for whoever is able to find thereto a way." (Quran 3:97, Sahih International)

Do Turkish citizens need a visa for Umrah? Yes — but unlike most of our readers, you can get yours online or at the airport. The Saudi tourist eVisa is valid for one year, allows multiple entries, and permits a cumulative stay of up to 90 days. It costs SAR 535, a fee that includes health cover of up to SAR 100,000. Apply in advance at visa.visitsaudi.com; the same visa is also available on arrival at Saudi international airports (VisaEntryRules; House of Saud). The tourist eVisa covers tourism, family visits, events and Umrah — but Hajj is excluded, and no tourist visa can be used for Hajj. Our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide sets out how this fee compares with the other visa types.

Route 1: Umrah on the tourist eVisa: This is the route most Turkish pilgrims now use. Apply at visa.visitsaudi.com before you travel, or take the visa on arrival, then book your Umrah permit through the Nusuk platform — the eVisa alone does not admit you to perform Umrah; the permit is booked separately in the Nusuk app, and Umrah on a tourist visa is performed outside the Hajj season (Wego Blog). You will also book your Rawdah permit in the same app when you visit Madinah — our Nusuk app guide walks through the setup step by step. Because you arrange everything yourself on this route, book your hotels and ground transport before you fly, and keep the confirmations with your passport. Travellers who prefer everything arranged for them can still book a complete programme through a Turkish Umrah company, which handles the visa, hotels and transfers together — the choice is genuinely yours, in a way it is not for pilgrims from non-eVisa countries.

Route 2: the dedicated Umrah visa through Nusuk: The alternative is a dedicated Umrah visa, and since July 2026 its headline form is the one-year multiple-entry Umrah visa issued via Nusuk: valid for 365 days from issue, with a cumulative stay of up to 90 days across the year, an Umrah permit required before each arrival, and the visa inactive during the Hajj season — from 1 Dhu al-Qi'dah to 13 Dhu al-Hijjah (VisaEntryRules). Pilgrims have been able to apply directly on the Nusuk Umrah service since 20 August 2025. One rule to plan around: since 10 June 2025, an Umrah visa is issued only once your accommodation and transport booking is documented and approved (VisaEntryRules; Şahinoğlu Turizm) — so fix your hotels and transfers first, then apply, rather than the other way round.

The 1448 AH season dates:

  • The last day for Umrah visa issuance in the 1448 AH season is 9 March 2027.
  • The last day to enter Saudi Arabia on an Umrah visa is 23 March 2027.
  • Every Umrah visitor must depart by 7 April 2027.
  • Neither the tourist eVisa nor the Umrah visa can be used for Umrah during the Hajj season itself, when the Haram is reserved for Hajj pilgrims.

Documents and vaccinations checklist:

  • Passport valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date.
  • The new chipped Turkish passport is mandatory — Saudi visas cannot be issued on old-style, non-chipped Turkish passports, so check your passport type before you book anything (Şahinoğlu Turizm).
  • Meningococcal ACWY vaccination, taken at least 10 days before arrival — the Saudi rule for all pilgrims, whichever visa you travel on. Carry the certificate in your hand luggage.
  • Your hotel and transport bookings — documented and approved before an Umrah visa is issued, and worth carrying in print on the eVisa route too.
  • A confirmed return ticket — airlines and immigration officers may ask to see it, whichever route you travel on.
  • Women of all ages may perform Umrah without a mahram.

Flights from Istanbul to Jeddah: Jeddah (King Abdulaziz International, JED) is the arrival airport for Makkah, and Istanbul (IST) is one of the best-connected departure points in this entire series of guides: about 12 nonstop flights a day link the two cities, with a direct flying time of about 3 hours 45 minutes to 3 hours 48 minutes. The nonstop carriers are Turkish Airlines, Saudia, flyadeal and flynas. Fares checked in November 2026 on KAYAK: a round trip starts from about US$234, with a typical round-trip range of US$249–327 and the cheapest nonstop at about US$260; one-way fares start from about US$129 (KAYAK) and about ₹11,313 on flynas (HappyFares, fares seen in the 30 days to 18 November 2026). Frequently asked questions:

  • Can Turkish citizens get a Saudi eVisa or visa on arrival? Yes. Turkey is among the 72 countries on the tourist eVisa list after the 5 November 2026 expansion. The eVisa is valid for one year, allows multiple entries and up to 90 days in total, costs SAR 535 including health cover up to SAR 100,000, and can be applied for at visa.visitsaudi.com or obtained on arrival at Saudi international airports.
  • Can I perform Umrah on the tourist eVisa? Yes, outside the Hajj season — but you must also book an Umrah permit through the Nusuk platform. The tourist eVisa never covers Hajj.
  • What passport do I need? A Turkish passport valid for at least six months beyond your arrival — and it must be the new chipped passport. Visas cannot be issued on old-style Turkish passports.
  • When does the Umrah season end in 1448 AH? Visa issuance ends 9 March 2027, the last entry is 23 March 2027, and all Umrah visitors must depart by 7 April 2027.
