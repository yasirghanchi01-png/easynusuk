Umrah from United States — Visa & Flights Guide

American pilgrims have one of the most flexible Umrah routes in the world: the United States is on the Saudi tourist eVisa list (Wego, updated 12 Nov 2026), and Saudia is the only non-stop operator between the United States and Saudi Arabia, flying non-stop to Jeddah from New York, Washington and Los Angeles. That means you can apply for a tourist eVisa online before you fly, get a visa on arrival, or still use the dedicated Umrah visa route when you prefer a package. This guide covers both routes, the 1448H season dates, the documents and health rules, and the non-stop and one-stop flights and fares. Travelling from Europe instead? Our newest companion guide, Umrah from France, covers the same eVisa route with a twice-daily non-stop Paris flight, and our Umrah Visa for UK & US Citizens guide walks through the same online application step by step.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Umrah to Umrah expiates what is between them." (Sahih al-Bukhari 1773, Sahih Muslim 1349)

Do American pilgrims need a visa for Umrah? Yes — but you can get yours online in advance or at the airport. The United States is on the Saudi tourist eVisa list, and the dedicated Umrah visa through Nusuk remains available when you prefer everything arranged in a package. Our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide sets out how the fees compare across the visa types.

Route 1: the tourist eVisa (or visa on arrival): Apply online at visa.visitsaudi.com. The eVisa costs SAR 535 in total, including the mandatory health insurance (about $143), is valid for 1 year with multiple entries, and allows up to 90 days per visit. Umrah is allowed on it outside the Hajj season; it is not valid for Hajj or work. A visa on arrival is available on the same terms for US passport holders at Saudi airports.

Route 2: the Umrah visa via Nusuk or an agent: Apply on the Nusuk platform (umrah.nusuk.sa) or through a licensed US travel agent. Since 20 July 2026 there is also a multiple-entry Umrah visa: valid for 365 days from issue with up to 90 days in total across all visits — each visit needs its own Nusuk service package and Umrah permit — and it is not usable during the Hajj season (1 Dhu al-Qi'dah to 13 Dhu al-Hijjah). Our Nusuk app guide walks through the setup and the permit booking step by step.

A route that does not exist — the Package eVisa: The instant Package eVisa, bundled with a licensed-agency package, is not available for the United States. It covers only seven countries — Jordan, Egypt, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Mexico and Pakistan — so Americans use the tourist eVisa, the visa on arrival, or the Umrah visa instead.

The 1448H season dates:

  • Umrah visa issuance ends 9 March 2027.
  • The last entry date is 23 March 2027.
  • All Umrah visa holders must depart by 7 April 2027.

Documents and health checklist:

  • Passport valid for at least six months at entry.
  • Meningococcal ACWY vaccination is mandatory, at least 10 days before travel, with the certificate ready to show.
  • Polio vaccination is not compulsory for travellers from the United States.
  • A yellow fever certificate is needed only if you are arriving from, or transiting, a yellow-fever risk country.
  • Women of all ages may travel for Umrah without a mahram — our Umrah for women travelling alone guide has the rules and practical detail.

Flights from the United States to Jeddah: Saudia is the only non-stop operator between the US and Saudi Arabia. New York JFK to Jeddah takes about 11h 35m over 6,354 miles (about 10,227 km), several times a week; Washington Dulles (IAD) to Jeddah is non-stop at about 11h 48m; and Los Angeles (LAX) to Jeddah is non-stop at about 16h 45m, three times a week. One-stop options via Doha, Dubai, Istanbul or London take roughly 15–18 hours. The cheapest non-stop fares start from about $856, typical returns run $868–$1,298, and one-stop returns start from about $356. All fares checked 20 Nov 2026, prices change daily. Jeddah is 8 hours ahead of New York in winter (EST) and 7 hours ahead in summer (EDT). On arrival, our Jeddah Airport to Makkah guide covers the onward taxi, bus and train options, and if your stay splits between the two holy cities, the Makkah to Madinah transport guide compares the Haramain train, bus and taxi, while our Madinah hotels guide helps you choose where to stay near the Prophet's Mosque.

What it costs: On the eVisa route you pay the SAR 535 visa fee yourself and book flights and hotels separately; on the Umrah visa route the visa comes inside the package. Flights are the biggest variable, then hotels near the Haram. See our general Umrah cost guide and the Makkah hotels guide before you book.

Step by step:

  1. Choose your dates inside the 1448H season.
  2. Pick the visa route — tourist eVisa online or on arrival, or the Umrah visa via Nusuk or a licensed US travel agent.
  3. Get the meningococcal ACWY vaccine at least 10 days before travel.
  4. Book your flights and hotels (or your package on the Umrah visa route).
  5. Book the free Umrah permit on the Nusuk app before entering Masjid al-Haram.
  6. Book the Rawdah permit too if you will visit Madinah.
  7. Pack using our Umrah packing list.

Preparing for the journey itself: With the visa and flights settled, the rest is preparation. Choose your dates with the best time to perform Umrah guide, which compares weather, crowds and cost across the year, and learn the supplications from Umrah Duas and Tawaf Duas before you fly, so the rites themselves feel familiar when you reach the Haram.

Frequently asked questions:

  • Is the United States on the Saudi tourist eVisa list? Yes (Wego, updated 12 Nov 2026). Apply online at visa.visitsaudi.com.
  • Can I perform Umrah on the tourist eVisa? Yes, outside the Hajj season. The eVisa costs SAR 535 including mandatory health insurance (about $143), lasts 1 year with multiple entries, and allows up to 90 days per visit; a visa on arrival is available on the same terms for US passport holders. It is not valid for Hajj or work.
  • Are there direct flights from the US to Jeddah? Yes — Saudia is the only non-stop operator: New York JFK (about 11h 35m, several times a week), Washington Dulles (about 11h 48m) and Los Angeles (about 16h 45m, three times a week).
  • Is the Package eVisa available for the United States? No. The Package eVisa covers only Jordan, Egypt, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Mexico and Pakistan.
  • When does the Umrah season end in 1448H? Visa issuance ends 9 March 2027, the last entry is 23 March 2027, and all Umrah visa holders must depart by 7 April 2027.
