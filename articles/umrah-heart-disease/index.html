Umrah with Heart Disease: Safe Travel Tips for Cardiac Patients

Short answer: Yes, most cardiac patients can perform Umrah safely if their condition is stable, with a doctor's clearance and a slower pace. The Saudi Ministry of Health says a cardiac patient can perform the rituals of Hajj and Umrah if his health condition is stable, provided he follows specific precautions.

Before you travel: See your cardiologist 4 to 6 weeks before departure to confirm you are fit to fly and fit for the walking Umrah involves. Ask for a written summary letter listing your diagnosis and every medicine, plus prescriptions for all of them. Carry the letter and prescriptions with you at all times, and check that your travel insurance covers a pre-existing heart condition emergency, because the insurance bundled with an Umrah visa mainly covers sudden emergencies.

Medication: Keep all heart medicines in their original labelled packaging. Split the supply between your hand luggage and a companion's bag so one lost suitcase cannot stop your treatment. Carry the day's doses on your body during the rites. Take extra stock for delays. If you take blood thinners, ask your doctor about the long flight, moving your legs during the flight, and compression socks.

During the rites: Tawaf is about 2 km of walking and Sa'i about 3 km, so pace yourself. Do the rites in the cooler hours, after Fajr or late at night, and rest between them. Use a wheelchair for Tawaf and Sa'i when you feel tired, the Ministry of Health explicitly allows this. Avoid the midday heat, drink water regularly, and follow a diet low in fats and salts as your doctor advised. Travel with family or friends who know your condition and where your medicines are.

Warning signs, go to the nearest health centre immediately: Any chest pain, pain spreading from the chest to the left arm, jaw, neck or stomach, shortness of breath, dizziness or feeling unbalanced, or excessive sweating. In Saudi Arabia the ambulance number is 997. Do not try to finish the rite first.
