Umrah with High Blood Pressure: Medication, Heat and Safety Tips

Short answer: Controlled high blood pressure does not stop you performing Umrah, but heat, long walking, broken sleep and salty restaurant food can push readings up. Prepare like you prepare the visa. Thousands of pilgrims with hypertension complete Umrah every year. The ones who struggle are usually the ones who treated the trip like a normal holiday and left their routine, and their medicines, to chance.

See your doctor first: Get a review 2 to 4 weeks before travel, with your dose confirmed for the physical load of Tawaf, Sa'i and walking to the Haram several times a day. Ask whether your medicines need any adjustment for the heat and the long days on your feet. Carry a doctor's letter listing your medicines by their generic names and doses, plus a written blood pressure log from home, so any doctor in Saudi Arabia can see your normal readings at a glance.

Medication discipline: Never skip a dose, especially on the morning of the flight, because airport stress plus a missed dose is the classic arrival day spike. Carry double the supply you need, in the original boxes, in your cabin bag, never in checked luggage. Shift your dose time gradually toward Saudi time over the first two days and confirm the timing with your doctor, because diuretics (water tablets) plus dehydration in Makkah heat are a real risk. Keep a paper card in your pouch with drug names and doses in English.

Beat the heat: Perform Tawaf and Sa'i in the cooler hours, after Fajr and late at night, when the marble is cooler and there is no direct sun. Rest between rituals like an appointment, not only when you are exhausted. Drink water before entering the Haram, use an umbrella in the sun, and sit in the shade whenever you have to wait. Use a wheelchair when you feel tired instead of pushing through, because a finished Umrah at a calm pace is far better than a crisis in the crowd.

Food and drink: Restaurant food in Saudi Arabia is often saltier than home cooking, so ask for less salt and avoid heavy salty meals and fast food. Choose grilled dishes, salads and fruit when you can. Limit tea and coffee, because caffeine raises blood pressure, and be careful with energy drinks. Drink at least 8 glasses of water a day, spread across the day, and more on days when you walk a lot. If your doctor has given you a daily fluid target, follow that instead.

Warning signs: Stop, sit down and seek help if you feel a severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, an irregular heartbeat or dizziness. Call the Saudi emergency number 997, or the health line 937. Hospitals near the Harams include King Abdul Aziz Hospital and King Faisal Hospital in Makkah and King Fahd Hospital in Madinah. Tell your group leader about your condition before the trip, so someone knows where your medicines are and which hospital to head for. Carry your doctor's letter whenever you go out.

After you return home: See your doctor again for a check-up. Travel, heat and broken sleep can shift your readings for a while, and your dose may need a small adjustment. Take the blood pressure log from your trip with you, because it shows your doctor exactly how your body handled the journey. Then rest properly for a few days before returning to your full routine.
