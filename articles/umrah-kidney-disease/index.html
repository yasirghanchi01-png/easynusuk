Umrah with Kidney Disease: Dialysis, Travel and Care Tips

Short answer: Yes, Umrah is possible for kidney patients, including patients on dialysis, with careful planning and a doctor's clearance. Saudi Arabia provides free haemodialysis for pilgrims in Makkah and Madinah, so treatment does not have to stop for the journey. What makes it work is preparation started months ahead, not arrangements made at the airport.

Before you travel: Tell your kidney doctor at least 6 months before travel, so they can review your condition and confirm you are fit to travel. Tell your dialysis unit at least 2 months before, so it can prepare your reports and treatment summary. Bring your consultant report, recent blood test results, Hepatitis B and C and HIV test results, a copy of your visa, and a GP letter listing all your medicines. Make several copies of everything, and keep one set in your hand luggage.

An important note about Hajj: Saudi Ministry of Health guidance for Hajj 1447H/2026 lists kidney failure needing dialysis among the conditions not compatible with the minimum physical ability required for Hajj, so dialysis patients are advised not to travel for Hajj. Umrah outside the Hajj season is the realistic option, and even then only if your own doctor clears you to go.

Dialysis in Makkah: King Abdul Aziz Hospital, also known as Zahir Hospital, provides dialysis for pilgrims and is about 15 minutes by taxi from Ajyad. King Faisal Hospital and Al Noor Hospital also provide dialysis for pilgrims in Makkah, so there is more than one option if one unit is full.

Dialysis in Madinah: King Fahd Hospital provides dialysis for pilgrims in Madinah and is about 20 to 25 minutes from the Prophet's Mosque. Plan your session times around the journey between the two cities, and tell both units your travel dates so no session is missed in between.

How booking your sessions works: On arrival, go to the hospital's Emergency Department first to open a file, taking your reports, visa and insurance details with you. Then go to the dialysis unit to book your sessions, and tell them the days and the frequency you need. The dialysis unit at King Abdul Aziz Hospital is closed on Fridays. In busy times such as Hajj and Ramadan, a same-day slot may not be available, so go a day early with your paperwork rather than waiting until the day a session is due.

During the trip: Keep your medicines in their original packaging with their prescriptions. Follow your doctor's advice on fluids and salt, even when Zamzam and hospitality make it tempting to drink freely. Avoid the midday heat, perform the rites in the cooler hours, and rest between them. Use a wheelchair when you feel tired. Travel with a companion, and if possible with an Arabic speaker, because admin staff may not speak English and an Arabic speaker helps a lot with hospital paperwork. Female patients should bring a female companion, as men may not be allowed into some waiting areas.

Warning signs, get care immediately: Breathlessness, swelling, chest pain, confusion, or very low urine output. Do not wait for your next booked session. The ambulance number in Saudi Arabia is 997.
