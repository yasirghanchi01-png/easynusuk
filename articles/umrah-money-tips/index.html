Umrah Money Tips: Cash, Cards and Riyal in Saudi Arabia

Short answer: Saudi Arabia is now largely cashless, but a pilgrim still needs some paper Riyals. Plan the mix before you fly, so money never distracts from worship. Decide at home how much of your spending money travels as cash, how much stays on a card, and who in the family carries what. Pilgrims who sort this out before departure rarely have to think about money again until they are home.

The Riyal and the 3.75 peg: The Saudi Riyal (SAR) is pegged to the US dollar at 3.75 Riyals to 1 dollar, a peg held since 1986. That makes mental maths easy: SAR 100 is about 27 dollars, SAR 375 is 100 dollars, and SAR 1,000 is about 267 dollars. Because the rate does not move, any exchange rate meaningfully worse than the peg is the dealer's margin, not the market. If a counter offers you far fewer Riyals per dollar than 3.75, walk to the next counter and compare.

Cards work almost everywhere: The mada national network plus Visa and Mastercard run on more than 1.4 million terminals across the Kingdom. Hotels, restaurants, pharmacies, supermarkets, malls, the Haramain train and ride-hailing apps all take cards, and contactless payment and Apple Pay are normal. Tell your bank before you travel so an overseas transaction is not blocked as suspicious, and check your foreign transaction fee at home, because a card that charges a fee on every purchase quietly adds up over a two week trip.

Where cash still rules: Tips for porters, cleaners and wheelchair pushers, usually 5 to 20 Riyals at a time. Some older street taxis still prefer cash, and so do small stalls selling dates, prayer rugs and tasbih, small eateries away from the centre, and laundry shops. None of these amounts is large, but they come up every day. Keep 500 to 1,500 Riyals in cash for these small payments, in small notes, and keep the rest of your money on your card.

Exchanging money: Exchange only a small amount at the airport, enough for a taxi and a SIM card, because airport counters give the worst rates. For larger amounts use licensed money exchange houses (sarafah) near the Haram in Makkah and Madinah, such as Al Rajhi Exchange and Enjaz, which give better rates. Check the day's rate before you exchange, use clean undamaged notes, and count the money at the counter. Ask for small denominations, 5, 10 and 50 Riyal notes, because small vendors may not have change for 100 or 500 Riyal notes.

ATMs: ATMs are plentiful near both Harams, run by Al Rajhi Bank, Saudi National Bank (SNB), Riyad Bank and Al Bilad Bank. Most Visa, Mastercard and UnionPay cards work in them. Machines usually dispense 100 and 500 Riyal notes, so break a large note at a supermarket when you can. Expect a Saudi operator fee of about 7 to 15 Riyals per foreign card withdrawal, plus your own bank's fee, so withdraw larger amounts less often instead of many small withdrawals. Use machines inside bank branches or malls where you can.

Keeping money safe: Split money between a waist pouch and your bag, so one loss never leaves you with nothing. Keep the bulk of your cash and your spare card in the hotel safe, and carry only what you need for the day. Keep receipts, check your banking app each evening, and report a lost card to your bank at once. Never hand your card to a stranger, and never let anyone help you at an ATM. If you travel as a family, agree on one shared emergency amount that everyone knows about.
