Umrah in Ramadan 2027 — Dates, Costs & Tips

Planning Umrah in Ramadan 2027 means planning around two calendars at once: Ramadan 1448 AH itself, and the end of the Umrah season, which closes in the middle of it. This guide is the 2027-specific companion to our general Umrah in Ramadan article — here you will find the expected dates table, the visa deadline that falls on Eid day itself, what peak-season costs and crowds really look like, and how to pace fasting, Taraweeh and the last ten nights. For the wider year-round picture, see the best time to perform Umrah guide.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Umrah in Ramadan is equivalent to Hajj" — and in one narration, "equivalent to Hajj with me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1782; Sahih Muslim 1256). The scholars explain that it equals Hajj in reward; it does not replace the obligatory Hajj for someone who later becomes able to perform it — see our Hajj 2027 guide for that journey.

Ramadan 2027 dates — all provisional: On Saudi Arabia's Umm al-Qura calendar, Ramadan 1448 AH is expected to run for 29 days, from Monday 8 February 2027 to Monday 8 March 2027, with Eid al-Fitr on Tuesday 9 March 2027. These are calculated dates only: the religious start and end of Ramadan are confirmed by the official moon sighting on the evening of 29 Sha'ban, and can shift by a day. Countries that follow local sighting — including Pakistan and India, which may begin on 8 or 9 February — can differ from Saudi Arabia by a day at either end, so Eid there may fall anywhere from 9 to 11 March. Build a day of flexibility into flights and hotel dates wherever you can.

The deadline warning — read this first: The Umrah season's last visa issuance date is 9 March 2027 — Eid al-Fitr day itself. In practice that means a Ramadan Umrah cannot be a last-minute decision: your visa must be applied for well before Ramadan ends, and because a standard Umrah visa must be used for entry within 30 days of issuance, the working window is tight. Apply and book early — ideally months ahead — and remember the final backstop: the last permitted entry is 23 March 2027, and all Umrah visitors must depart by 7 April 2027. Check the fee and processing picture in our Umrah Visa Fees 2026 guide before you commit.

Costs in Ramadan 2027 — the peak of the year: Ramadan is the most expensive Umrah season, and the last ten nights are the most expensive days within it. Flights into Jeddah and Madinah and rooms near the Haram reach their highest prices of the year, and the closest hotels sell out first. There is no honest fixed price for a Ramadan 2027 package — it depends on your departure country, hotel distance and how early you book — so use our Umrah cost breakdown for the baseline budget, then expect Ramadan, and especially the last ten nights, to sit well above it. The two levers that actually move the price are booking 5–6 months ahead and choosing the first half of Ramadan over the last ten nights if your intention allows. Compare areas before paying with our Makkah hotels and Madinah hotels guides.

Crowds, Taraweeh and Qiyam: Expect the fullest Haram of the year. The Grand Mosque fills to the streets for Maghrib, Isha and Taraweeh, so arrive around 90 minutes early to pray inside rather than in the outer courtyards. Flip your day around: sleep after Fajr and again after Dhuhr, and give the nights to Taraweeh, Qiyam, Qur'an and du'a — our Umrah duas collection will help you prepare what to recite. You still need one Nusuk Umrah permit for each performance of Umrah, booked in the app as soon as your dates are fixed; Ramadan slots go fastest of all — the Nusuk app guide shows the booking steps. First-timers should read the first-timers' guide alongside this one, and pack for long nights and warm days with the packing list.

The last ten nights and Laylat al-Qadr: The last ten nights run from the evening of 27 February to 8 March 2027 (provisional). In them falls Laylat al-Qadr, the night better than a thousand months (Quran 97:3), sought on the odd nights — the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th and 29th. Many pilgrims aim specifically for these nights in Makkah or Madinah, which is why they are the most crowded and costly of the season. Increase du'a and night prayer, rest honestly in the day, and do not try to perform Umrah itself on the most packed nights if a quieter night in the same stay is possible.

Fasting while travelling: Allah says: "…whoever among you is ill or on a journey, then an equal number of other days [must be made up]" (Quran 2:185) — the concession to postpone fasts while travelling exists, and travellers differ in what they choose: many pilgrims fast in the holy cities and find it manageable with the flipped Ramadan schedule, while others use the concession on the travel days themselves and make the fasts up later. Decide with a scholar you trust before you go, not on the plane. Either way, eat a real Suhoor, break the fast simply — dates and Zamzam are distributed in the Haram — and hydrate steadily between Iftar and Suhoor.

Rawdah in Ramadan: A visit to the Rawdah in Madinah during Ramadan is booked exactly as at any other time — only through a Rawdah permit on the Nusuk app — but demand is at its yearly peak and slots disappear quickly. Book the moment your window opens, be flexible about which day of your Madinah stay you take, and keep checking the app for released slots.

Frequently asked questions:

  • When is Ramadan 2027? Expected Monday 8 February to Monday 8 March 2027 (29 days, Umm al-Qura calendar), with Eid al-Fitr on Tuesday 9 March 2027. All dates are provisional until confirmed by moon sighting, and Pakistan and India may differ by a day.
  • Can I still get an Umrah visa during Ramadan 2027? Yes, but not at the last minute: visa issuance for the 1448 season ends on 9 March 2027, the last entry is 23 March 2027, and a visa must be entered on within 30 days of issue. Apply well before Ramadan ends — in practice, book the whole trip months ahead.
  • Which part of Ramadan 2027 is busiest? The last ten nights, from about 27 February to 8 March, when pilgrims gather for Qiyam and Laylat al-Qadr. They are also the most expensive nights of the year for flights and hotels near the Haram.
  • Does Umrah in Ramadan replace Hajj? No. It equals Hajj in reward (Sahih al-Bukhari 1782; Sahih Muslim 1256), but the obligation of Hajj remains for whoever becomes able to perform it.
