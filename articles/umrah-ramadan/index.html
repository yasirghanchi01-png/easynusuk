Umrah in Ramadan — Virtues, Booking & Tips

Of all the months in which to stand before the Kaaba, none carries a promise like Ramadan. Umrah at any time of year wipes away sins and poverty, but an Umrah performed while fasting, praying Taraweeh and chasing Laylatul Qadr is something pilgrims save for years to experience. Here is what the Prophet ﷺ said about it, what it does and does not replace, and how to plan the trip well. If this is your first journey, read our Umrah for First-Timers guide alongside this one.

The promise: The Prophet ﷺ said: "Umrah in Ramadan is equivalent to Hajj" — and in one narration, "equivalent to Hajj with me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1782; Sahih Muslim 1256). He said this to a woman of the Ansar who had missed Hajj with him because her family had only two camels, and he directed her to perform Umrah in Ramadan instead.

Equal in reward — not a replacement for Hajj: Read the hadith carefully. The scholars explain that Ramadan Umrah equals Hajj in reward, not in what it discharges. It does not replace the obligatory Hajj: a Muslim who performs Umrah in Ramadan and later becomes able to perform Hajj still owes Hajj, exactly as before. IslamQA (fatwa 104926) states this plainly, and the scholars of the four madhhabs hold that the virtue in the hadith is general — it is not limited to that one woman, nor to someone who missed Hajj; it applies to anyone who performs Umrah in Ramadan. So go for the multiplied reward, and keep Hajj itself on your plans for when Allah enables it — our Hajj 2027 guide shows how that journey is booked.

Planning a Ramadan Umrah — what is different:

  • Book 5–6 months ahead. Ramadan is the busiest Umrah season of the year. Flights and hotels — above all the hotels near the Haram — fill fast and prices climb steeply, especially for the last ten nights. Sort your visa early too: see Umrah Visa 2026 for the visa types and Umrah Visa Fees 2026 for what it costs.
  • You still need a Nusuk Umrah permit. Ramadan changes nothing here: you need one Umrah permit for each performance of Umrah, booked in the Nusuk app, and you show the permit's QR code at the checkpoint. Book your slot as soon as your dates are fixed — Ramadan slots go quickly. Our Nusuk App Guide walks through the booking step by step.
  • Taraweeh crowds. The Haram fills to the streets for Maghrib, Isha and Taraweeh. Arrive about 90 minutes before Maghrib or Isha to secure a place inside; arriving at the adhan often means praying in the outer courtyards or the road.
  • Flip your day around. Rest and sleep in the daytime — after Fajr and again after Dhuhr if you can — and give the nights to worship: Taraweeh, Tahajjud, Qur'an and du'a. Fighting the crowds on no sleep helps no one.
  • The last ten nights. These are the peak of the month and the peak of the crowds. In them falls Laylatul Qadr, better than a thousand months — increase du'a and i'tikaf-style devotion, and expect the Haram to be at its fullest on the odd nights.
  • Iftar and Suhoor. Break your fast simply — dates and Zamzam are distributed in the Haram, and many pilgrims iftar in the courtyard before Maghrib prayer. Eat a real Suhoor before Fajr even if you must wake for it; you will need the strength for a day of fasting and walking. Keep iftar light if you plan to stand long in Taraweeh.
  • Pack differently for Ramadan. Take a lighter Ihram — the nights are long in worship and the days warm. Pack dates for iftar on the move and a refillable water bottle for Zamzam and for keeping hydrated between Iftar and Suhoor. Our full Umrah Packing List covers everything else.

Quick questions:

  • Does Ramadan Umrah replace Hajj? No. It equals Hajj in reward, as the Prophet ﷺ said (Sahih al-Bukhari 1782; Sahih Muslim 1256), but it does not lift the obligation of Hajj. If Hajj becomes possible for you later, it remains due — IslamQA fatwa 104926 confirms this.
  • Do I need a Nusuk permit in Ramadan? Yes. You need one Umrah permit per performance of Umrah, booked in the Nusuk app, and you present its QR code at the entrance — Ramadan is no exception, and slots fill faster than in any other month.
  • When should I book? Five to six months before Ramadan if you can. Hotels near the Haram sell out first and prices rise sharply, above all for the last ten nights — and your Nusuk Umrah slot and visa should be arranged just as early.
