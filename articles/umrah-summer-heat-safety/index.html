Umrah in Summer Heat: Staying Safe in Makkah and Madinah

Summer Umrah, from June to August, is the cheapest and quietest time to travel, and many families choose it for the school holidays. The trade off is serious heat. Makkah's average daytime high in June is 43.9°C, and on the open marble of the Haram courtyard it feels hotter still. The heat is a planning problem, not a reason to cancel: with the right schedule, water, and rest, a summer Umrah is perfectly doable.

How hot it really gets: June averages a high of 43.9°C and a low of 29.3°C; July averages 43.2°C and 29.8°C; August averages 42.9°C and 30.1°C (World Meteorological Organization climate normals 1991-2020, Makkah station). The highest temperature ever recorded in Makkah is 51.8°C. Madinah is similar, with August averaging 43.8°C by day. Nights stay near 30°C, so there is little relief after dark. The white marble around the Kaaba reflects the sun and feels hotter than the air temperature, and the walk between hotel and mosque is where most people feel it.

Plan your rites around the sun: Do Tawaf and Sa'i before Fajr or after Maghrib and Isha, when the air is coolest and the courtyards are shaded. Keep 11 AM to 4 PM for rest, prayer inside, and sleep. The Haram interior and the Sa'i gallery are fully air conditioned, so indoor worship is comfortable at any hour. If you must cross the courtyard at midday, walk in the shade of the colonnades, carry water, and keep it short.

Drink before you feel thirsty: In 43°C heat you lose water fast, and thirst arrives late. Carry a refillable bottle and sip through the day. Zamzam water is available throughout both Harams. Pack oral rehydration salt sachets from home and use one if you have been sweating heavily. Go easy on tea, coffee, and soft drinks, which do not replace water.

What to wear and carry: Light, loose, breathable clothes in light colours. A white umbrella for the walk to the Haram, a hat or cap outside Ihram, sunglasses, and sunscreen. In Ihram, use unscented sunscreen and unscented toiletries, because scented products are not allowed in the state of Ihram. A small hand fan or a damp cooling towel helps on the courtyard. Wear comfortable sandals with grip, because hot marble is slippery when wet. Our full Umrah packing list covers everything else to pack.

Look after the vulnerable: Elderly pilgrims, small children, pregnant women, and anyone with heart, kidney, or breathing problems feel the heat first. Keep them in the shade, shorten their time outdoors, and consider a wheelchair for Tawaf and Sa'i on the air conditioned upper floors. Children dehydrate faster than adults and complain later, so offer them water on a schedule, not when they ask. See our guides to Umrah for elderly and disabled pilgrims and Umrah with kids for more detail.

Know the warning signs: Heat exhaustion comes first: tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea, heavy sweating with pale clammy skin, cramps, and strong thirst. Move the person to a cool place at once, lay them down, loosen clothing, cool the skin with water, and give small sips of water. If they feel better within 30 minutes, they are recovering. Heatstroke is a medical emergency: a very high temperature, hot skin that has stopped sweating, a fast heartbeat, fast breathing, confusion, a seizure, or loss of consciousness. Call for help immediately and cool the person with a wet sheet or cold water while you wait.

Emergency numbers to save: 911 is the unified emergency number in Makkah and Madinah. 997 reaches the Saudi Red Crescent ambulance anywhere in the Kingdom. 998 is Civil Defence. Save all three in your phone before you travel.

If your dates are flexible, check our guide to the best time to go for Umrah, consider a day trip to Taif, in the mountains near Makkah, where the air is noticeably cooler, or plan the main rites for the night hours and keep the days for rest. Summer Umrah rewards the pilgrim who respects the sun: early mornings, late nights, water in hand, and the midday hours spent in the cool of the Haram.

Sources: World Meteorological Organization climate normals 1991-2020 (Makkah station); NHS guidance on heat exhaustion and heatstroke.
