Umrah Visa for Children: Age Rules, Documents and Fees for Families

Children of any age can go for Umrah, from newborns to teenagers, and every child travels as a separate pilgrim. That means a separate passport, a separate visa and, from the age of five, a separate Nusuk permit. There is no child discount on the visa fee, and no child rides on a parent's papers. This guide covers the age rules, the documents each child needs, the fees, and the points families from Pakistan most often get wrong.

Age rules: what changes at five. There is no minimum age for Umrah itself. The Ministry of Hajj and Umrah FAQ says the minimum age for issuing an Umrah visa or permit is five years, and children aged five and above may perform Umrah with their guardians. In practice, children under five travel with their parents and enter both mosques with them, but they cannot be booked into their own Nusuk Umrah permit slot. From age five, each child needs their own Umrah permit, added as a companion on a parent's Nusuk account with the name, date of birth and passport number exactly as in the passport. A Rawdah permit is also needed for anyone aged five and above who wants to pray in the Rawdah in Madinah. Under eighteens must travel with a parent or guardian, and airlines will not accept an under eighteen travelling alone to Saudi Arabia, even with a visa.

The visa and what it costs. Every child, including infants, needs their own Umrah visa. The visa fee is SAR 300 per person, plus mandatory medical insurance, and the same fee applies to a child as to an adult. Families usually apply through a licensed Umrah company, which handles the visa for the whole family together, so the children are approved with the parents. A note of caution for the youngest children: the Ministry FAQ wording on visas for under fives does not fully match how families actually travel, and the visa type an under five receives can depend on the operator and the Saudi Umrah company. Before you pay, ask your operator in writing which visa your youngest child will travel on.

Documents each child needs. Your operator will usually ask for: the child's own passport, valid for at least six months from travel, because children cannot travel on a parent's passport; the NADRA B-Form (Child Registration Certificate) or birth certificate, which links the child to the parents; a recent passport size photo on a white background; each parent's CNIC and passport copy; and the vaccination certificates for the child's age. Under the Saudi Ministry of Health Umrah health rules for 1447H, polio vaccination is required for everyone arriving from Pakistan, regardless of age, so babies need it too. The meningitis ACYW vaccine is also required, given at least ten days before arrival. Keep copies of the child's passport and visa on your phone as well as in paper.

Ihram and the rites for children. For a child too young to understand, usually under about seven, the parent makes the intention of ihram on the child's behalf and guides them through Tawaf and Sa'i. Boys can wear the two unstitched ihram cloths if they can manage them, and very young boys can wear simple clean clothes. Girls wear their normal modest clothes. An Umrah performed before puberty does not count as the obligatory one, so the child will need to perform it again as an adult. That does not make the trip any less rewarding: the child shares in the reward, and the family worships together.

Travelling with one parent or a relative. If only one parent is travelling, or a grandparent or other relative is bringing the child, carry proof of the relationship, such as the B-Form, and a consent letter from the other parent if your agent asks for it. Under eighteens must be accompanied, so the child must be on the same booking and the same visa group as the accompanying adult. In the Haram, use the free identification wristband machines at King Abdulaziz Gate 1 and King Fahd Gate 79, which print a band with your contact details so staff can reunite you if you are separated, and keep a laminated card in the child's pocket with your name, hotel and phone number in English and Arabic.
