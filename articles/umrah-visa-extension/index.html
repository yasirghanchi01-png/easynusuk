Can You Extend an Umrah Visa? Rules, Overstay Fines and What to Do Instead

The short answer is no. An Umrah visa cannot be extended. Your visa carries two separate dates, and confusing them is the mistake that causes most overstays. The first is the entry window: under the current 2026 rules, a single-entry Umrah visa must be used to enter Saudi Arabia within 30 days of the date it is issued, reduced from 90 days in earlier years. The second is the permitted stay: once inside, you may stay for up to 90 days, matching the length of your Umrah programme. On top of both sits the season deadline for 1448 AH: the Umrah season opened on 31 May 2026, the last date to enter is 23 March 2027, and every Umrah visa holder must depart by 7 April 2027, even if days remain on the visa.

Single-entry or multiple-entry: A single-entry Umrah visa is for one pilgrimage trip, with 30 days to enter and up to 90 days of stay after entry. A multiple-entry Umrah visa, launched in July 2026, is valid for one year from issuance, but the stay is 90 days in total across the whole year, not 90 days per visit. A multiple-entry holder who leaves and re-enters draws on the same 90-day balance. The Saudi tourist eVisa used for Umrah also cannot be extended: once your permitted days are used, you need a new visa.

What happens if you overstay: Saudi Arabia treats overstays seriously. The Ministry of Interior has warned that staying after a visa expires can bring fines of up to SAR 50,000, imprisonment of up to six months, and deportation, followed by a future entry ban. The violation is recorded against the passport in the Jawazat system and surfaces in every future Saudi visa assessment. The same rules apply to children, and Umrah companies that fail to report overstaying pilgrims can be fined too.

Are there any exceptions? Routine extensions do not exist. In genuine emergencies, such as hospitalisation or being medically unfit to travel, the authorities may grant a temporary permit. In 2026, Saudi Arabia also allowed holders of expired visit, Umrah and transit visas who were stranded by regional disruption to extend or leave without penalty through Absher. These are exceptional measures, not a service you can apply for. If illness, a cancelled flight or another emergency means you cannot leave on time, contact the General Directorate of Passports (Jawazat) before your visa expires, rather than waiting. Coming forward voluntarily is consistently treated better than being discovered at the airport.

What to do instead of extending: Plan your dates before you apply, and apply only once your travel dates are confirmed. If you need more time, the lawful path is to leave before your visa or the season deadline runs out and apply for a new visa when you are eligible. A multiple-entry holder may exit and re-enter within the remaining allowance. Note that family and personal visit visas work differently: the host can usually apply for an extension through Absher for a fee and with valid medical insurance, but that route does not apply to an Umrah visa. Before you travel, check the exact validity and stay printed on your own visa, and verify the current rules on the official Saudi eVisa portal and Nusuk, because the rules can change between seasons.
