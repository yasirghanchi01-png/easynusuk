Umrah on a Layover: The 96-Hour Saudi Transit Visa Explained

Short answer: Yes. If your flight connects through Saudi Arabia on Saudia or flynas, you can leave the airport, travel to Makkah and perform Umrah on the free Saudi stopover visa, as long as your layover is no more than 96 hours and you hold a booked Umrah slot on Nusuk before you travel. The rules are strict, so this guide explains them one by one. Travelling on a normal holiday instead? Start with our full Umrah visa guide.

What the visa is: The stopover (transit) visa lets you stay in Saudi Arabia for up to 96 hours, four days. It is single entry and cannot be extended. Once issued, it stays valid for entry for 90 days from the date of issue. The visa itself is free, but you still pay an application fee of about $10.50 plus mandatory medical insurance of about $29, roughly $39.50 in total. It covers tourism and Umrah, but it does not cover Hajj, and you get only one stopover per booking.

Who can get it: Only passengers booked on Saudia or flynas, on a single connecting ticket where Saudi Arabia is the stop in the middle and your final destination is a third country. No other airline offers this visa; fly with anyone else and you need a separate transit visa arranged in advance. You need a passport valid for at least six months, a confirmed onward ticket, and a visa or residence permit for your final destination if that country requires one. Compare the cost with a standard visa in our guide to visa fees.

How to apply: You apply during the airline booking flow, and it takes about three minutes. On Saudia, apply in the "Seats and Extras" section; on flynas, apply through its stopover visa page. The application goes to the national visa platform of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and the visa and insurance papers arrive by email. Saudia asks for a square photo, 200x200 pixels and no larger than 20KB. Saudia also includes one free hotel night, claimed through Saudia Holidays after payment; flynas does not advertise one. An issued visa does not guarantee entry: passport control can still refuse you.

Umrah on this visa: The visa alone is not enough to perform Umrah. You must register on the Nusuk app or website and book your Umrah slot before you travel, and book a Rawdah permit the same way if you plan to visit Madinah. Our step-by-step Nusuk Umrah permit guide walks through the booking. The current 1448 AH Umrah season runs from 31 May 2026 to 7 April 2027, so finish your trip well before the season closes and check the cut-off dates on Nusuk first. You can also check your visa status online before departure.

Layovers under 12 hours: Short connections follow different rules. Saudia allows transit without a visa for up to 12 hours if you stay airside, inside the airport. But separate tickets, collecting your bags, or leaving the airside area can all require entry permission. A short layover is not a free Umrah trip: without the stopover visa and a Nusuk booking, you stay in the terminal.

A realistic 96-hour plan: Land in Jeddah and head straight to Makkah; our Jeddah airport to Makkah guide covers the Haramain high-speed train and taxi options. Perform Umrah on your first day while you are fresh, then rest. If time allows, add a day trip to Madinah for Ziyarah and a Rawdah visit with your booked permit. Be back at the airport well before the 96 hours run out: the clock starts when you enter the country.

Mistakes to avoid: Booking two separate tickets and assuming the stopover visa applies, when it only works on a single connecting ticket. Assuming any airline qualifies, when only Saudia and flynas offer it. Travelling without a Nusuk Umrah booking. Overstaying the 96 hours, which can bring fines and problems on future trips. And trying to use the stopover visa for Hajj, which it never covers.
