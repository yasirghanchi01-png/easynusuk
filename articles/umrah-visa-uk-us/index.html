Umrah Visa for UK & US Citizens — eVisa Steps

Quick answer: If you hold a passport from the UK, the US or one of the other eligible countries, you do not need an agent, an embassy visit or a paper application to get permission for Umrah. Saudi Arabia's tourist eVisa, applied for online at the official portal visa.visitsaudi.com, covers Umrah outside the Hajj season. It is valid for one year, allows multiple entries, and permits stays of up to 90 days at a time. Most applications are decided within 24–72 hours. One step remains after the visa arrives: registering on Nusuk and booking the free Umrah permit, because the visa alone does not book your Umrah. This guide covers who is eligible, what it really costs, the application step by step, and the season dates that matter. Once the visa is sorted, our Umrah Cost 2026 guide shows what the rest of the trip adds up to.

Who can use the eVisa: Citizens of 66 countries can apply, including the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Australia, most European Union states, Japan, South Korea, Singapore and Malaysia. The full country list sits on the portal itself, and it changes from time to time, so check your nationality there before anything else. The eVisa is a tourist visa that permits Umrah; it does not permit Hajj, paid work or study, and it cannot be converted into another visa type inside the Kingdom. If your country is not on the list, do not give up on the trip — the dedicated Umrah visa route further down this page was built for you.

What the eVisa gives you: One approval covers a full year of travel. You may enter Saudi Arabia multiple times within that year and stay up to 90 days on each visit, which makes the eVisa the natural choice for pilgrims who hope to return — for a second Umrah, or for Ramadan — without applying again. Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your travel dates; if it expires sooner, renew it before you apply, because the eVisa is issued against the passport you apply with. Medical insurance is not optional and not separate: every eVisa carries mandatory health insurance, assigned to you during the application and included in what you pay at checkout.

Because the insurance premium differs by insurer, two pilgrims can pay slightly different totals for the same visa. Treat every figure in this table — and every figure quoted by any agent — as an estimate: the total shown on the portal's checkout screen, before you pay, is the final truth.

How to apply — step by step:

  • Step 1 — Check your passport. Confirm it has at least six months' validity beyond your return date, and that the name on it is the name you will use everywhere else.
  • Step 2 — Go to the official portal. Open visa.visitsaudi.com and create an account with an email address you actually read — the decision and the eVisa itself arrive by email.
  • Step 3 — Start the application and choose the eVisa. Select the tourist eVisa and confirm your nationality is on the eligible list.
  • Step 4 — Enter your details exactly as printed in the passport. Names, passport number and dates must match letter for letter; a mismatch is the most common cause of delay.
  • Step 5 — Add your travel information and photo. Give your expected travel dates and upload a recent photograph that meets the portal's size and background requirements.
  • Step 6 — Review the assigned medical insurance. The portal attaches a policy from an approved Saudi insurer; its premium is added to your total.
  • Step 7 — Pay by card and submit. Check the full total on the checkout screen first — that figure is the one that counts — then pay and note your application reference.
  • Step 8 — Wait for the email, then print. Decisions typically take 24–72 hours, and many arrive within 6–12 hours. Print a paper copy of the eVisa and keep the PDF on your phone as well.

Visa on arrival — the alternative: Eligible nationals can instead obtain a visa on arrival at Saudi airports for about SAR 480, insurance included. There is also a wider door: holders of a valid US, UK or Schengen visa that has already been used to enter the country that issued it can get a visa on arrival regardless of their own nationality. The eVisa is still the calmer option for most pilgrims — it removes the airport queue, the card-terminal risk and any doubt before you board — but the arrival route is a genuine fallback if your plans change at the last minute.

After the visa — Nusuk registration is mandatory: A visa gets you into the Kingdom; it does not book your Umrah. Before you travel, register on nusuk.sa or in the Nusuk app and book the free Umrah permit for the day and time you plan to perform Umrah, and the Rawdah appointment for Madinah. Do this as soon as your dates are fixed — permits are released in batches and the popular times go first. With visa and permits in hand, work through our Umrah Packing List and choose your travel dates with our Best Time to Perform Umrah guide.

Health rule — do not leave it late: Pilgrims must show a meningitis ACWY vaccination received at least 10 days before travel. Book the vaccine as soon as your trip is likely, not the week you fly, and carry the certificate with your passport. Requirements are checked, and a certificate dated too close to departure can stop a journey at the airport.

Women travelling without a Mahram: Under the current rules, women aged 18 and over may travel for Umrah without a Mahram, and the eVisa application asks for no Mahram details. Women under 18 travel with a parent or guardian, as do all minors.

The 1448H Umrah season — key dates: Dedicated Umrah visa issuance for the 1448H season resumed on 31 May 2026. The last Umrah visa will be issued on 9 March 2027, the last entry on an Umrah visa is 23 March 2027, and all pilgrims who entered on an Umrah visa must leave by 7 April 2027, ahead of the Hajj season. If you are travelling anywhere near those closing dates, build your itinerary backwards from them and leave yourself margin — a missed deadline is not something an airline or an agent can fix at the airport.

Not on the eVisa list? The dedicated Umrah visa: Pilgrims whose nationality is not among the 66 can apply for a dedicated Umrah visa through the Nusuk Umrah platform at umrah.nusuk.sa. This route works differently: your hotel and transport bookings must be logged in Nusuk as part of the application, usually through an authorised Umrah company, and the issued visa must be used to enter Saudi Arabia within 30 days of issuance — so apply once your bookings are real, not months ahead on a hope. The Umrah permit and Rawdah bookings then follow in Nusuk exactly as they do for eVisa holders.

Please note — check the official portal before you book: Visa fees, insurance assignment and the eligible-country list all change, sometimes with little notice. This guide reflects the position checked in November 2026, but visa.visitsaudi.com on the day you apply is the final truth — if any figure or rule there differs from what you read here or anywhere else, the portal wins.

Quick questions:

  • Can UK and US citizens really perform Umrah on a tourist eVisa? Yes. The Saudi tourist eVisa covers Umrah outside the Hajj season for citizens of the 66 eligible countries, the UK and US included. You still need to register on Nusuk and book the free Umrah permit before you perform Umrah — the visa is entry permission, the permit is your Umrah booking.
  • How long does the eVisa take to come through? Typically 24–72 hours, and often within 6–12 hours. Apply at least a week before you fly anyway: a passport-detail mismatch or a photo rejection is easy to fix with days in hand and stressful without them.
  • What if my country is not on the eVisa list? Use the dedicated Umrah visa route via umrah.nusuk.sa, with your hotel and transport bookings logged in Nusuk as part of the application. Enter within 30 days of the visa being issued, and observe the season deadlines — for 1448H, the last Umrah visa is issued on 9 March 2027.
