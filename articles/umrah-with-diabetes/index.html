Umrah with Diabetes: Medication, Insulin and Safety Tips for Pilgrims

Diabetes does not stop you from performing Umrah. Every week of the year, pilgrims with diabetes complete their Umrah safely. What it does demand is planning. The heat, the long walks, the changed meal times and the crowded Haram can all push blood sugar up or down, so the pilgrims who do best are the ones who prepare weeks before they travel. This guide covers the practical steps. It is general guidance only, and your own doctor's plan always comes first.

Start with your doctor, 4 to 6 weeks before: Book a review appointment four to six weeks before departure. Ask for a fitness check, an HbA1c test to set your baseline, and a clear plan for adjusting your insulin or tablets during the pilgrimage, because the extra walking often means doses need to come down. A common approach is to reduce insulin before a long walk, but never change a dose on your own. Ask your doctor for three pieces of paper: a short summary of your condition and treatment, a medication list that uses generic names rather than brand names, because brands differ between countries, and a signed letter on letterhead covering any injectable medicines, needles or devices you carry. Keep one copy in your hand luggage, one in your day bag, and photos of everything on your phone.

Packing your diabetes kit: Pack a dedicated bag that stays in your hand luggage and never leaves your side: all medicines in their original labelled packaging, with about 30 percent extra in case of delays, your glucometer with plenty of test strips and lancets, glucose tablets, and snacks such as dates or energy bars for low blood sugar. If you use insulin, carry it in a cooler bag. Never put medicines in checked luggage, where bags go missing and temperatures swing. Loose pills in an unmarked organiser are exactly what customs officers are trained to question, so keep everything labelled and declare it with your doctor's letter.

Insulin in the Saudi heat: Unopened insulin must stay between 2 and 8 degrees Celsius, and insulin in use must stay below 30 degrees. In Makkah that takes care: use the hotel fridge for your stock, carry only the pen or vial you need for the day, keep it in a cooler bag, and never leave it in a hot car or in direct sun. Ask the hotel in advance about fridge access in your room. If your insulin has been sitting in heat above 30 degrees for hours, treat it as spoiled and do not use it.

Tawaf, Sa'i and low blood sugar: Tawaf is about 2 kilometres of walking and Sa'i about 3 kilometres, so check your blood sugar before you start and eat some extra carbohydrate, such as bread, before Tawaf. Carry glucose tablets or dates in your pocket. The warning signs of low blood sugar are sweating, shaking, a fast heartbeat, hunger and confusion. If they appear, stop and treat it at once with 2 to 3 dates, a spoon of honey, half a glass of juice or glucose tablets, then check your sugar again after 15 minutes. Tell your travelling companions in advance what low blood sugar looks like for you and where your glucose is kept. If you cannot manage the distance on foot, a wheelchair for Tawaf and Sa'i is fully valid.

Heat, water and feet: Heat and dehydration push blood sugar up and raise the risk of serious problems, so drink water and Zamzam often, rest in the shade between rites, and avoid the midday sun where you can. Check your feet every night: wash them, look for cuts or blisters, and wear comfortable shoes that are already broken in. Treat any small wound before you travel, because a small foot injury can become serious on a pilgrimage. Carry a medical ID card or bracelet that says you have diabetes, and travel insurance that covers it.

Food, timing and the journey home: Meal times will shift around prayers, so keep snacks in your day bag for delayed meals. Dates are ideal: quick sugar when you need it, and easy to carry. When you return home, book a check with your doctor within a week, because doses that were changed for the journey usually need changing back. Above all, do not let the worry take over the worship. With a doctor's plan, a packed kit and companions who know what to do, Umrah with diabetes is entirely possible.
