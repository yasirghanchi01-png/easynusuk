Umrah with Kids — Complete Family Guide

Quick answer: There is no minimum age for Umrah — a baby can be carried through every rite, and families do it every day of the year. The paperwork line sits at age five: the Nusuk Umrah permit starts at five, and children under five enter both mosques with their parents without a permit of their own. Every child, infants included, needs their own passport and visa, and everyone under 18 must travel with a parent or guardian. Beyond the paperwork, a family Umrah succeeds or fails on pacing: short visits, cool hours, a hotel near the Haram and a carrier you trust. This guide covers the rules first, then the age-by-age practice. If grandparents are coming too, pair it with our Umrah for Elderly Parents guide — the same gentle rhythm serves both ends of the family.

Children in Umrah — the religious basis: Taking children is not a modern concession; it is established practice from the Prophet's ﷺ own time. Ibn Abbas narrated that a woman lifted a child toward the Prophet ﷺ and asked, "Is there Hajj for this one?" He replied: "Yes, and you will have a reward" (Sahih Muslim 1336a). For a child below the age of discernment — generally under seven — the parent forms the intention of Ihram on the child's behalf and guides them through the rites; older children make their own intention with your help. One point to hold clearly: an Umrah performed before puberty is valid and rewarded, but it does not count as the obligatory one — your child will perform Umrah again as an adult with the duty fully their own. Nothing about that diminishes the trip: the reward is real, and so is the memory you are planting.

Permits and paperwork, child by child:

  • Passport and visa: every child — including a newborn — travels on their own passport with their own visa. There is no "added to a parent's passport" route.
  • Nusuk permit: children aged five and over need an Umrah permit booked on Nusuk like any other pilgrim; under-fives need no permit and enter Masjid al-Haram and the Prophet's Mosque with their parents.
  • Supervision: under-18s must be accompanied by a parent or guardian throughout the trip.
  • ID wristband: put a wristband on every young child with a parent's name and mobile number, and agree a fixed meeting point. In a crowd of a million, this five-minute job is the one that matters.

Vaccines — the rule covers children too: The meningitis ACWY requirement is not adults-only. Each child must be vaccinated at least 10 days before arrival, with a conjugate vaccine given within the last 5 years or a polysaccharide vaccine within the last 3 years. Book the children's appointments when you book your own, and carry every certificate together in one documents folder — checks happen, and a missing child's certificate holds up the whole family.

Strollers — where they work and where they do not: Strollers are banned on the ground-floor Mataf, the open floor circling the Kaaba, for safety and crowd density — no exceptions are made at the barrier, so do not plan a ground-floor Tawaf around one. They are generally permitted in the Mas'a corridor where Sa'i is walked, on the upper floors and in the outer courtyards. The kit that works, in practice: a soft carrier or sling for Tawaf and Sa'i, keeping your hands free and your child against you in the densest crowds, and a compact, foldable stroller for everything else — the hotel-to-Haram walk, the courtyards, and the long, calmer spaces of Madinah. If you only bring one, bring the carrier.

A facility most families never hear of — the Children's Nursery Centers: Inside the Grand Mosque's third expansion, the mosque runs Children's Nursery Centers staffed by trained carers, with meals and sleeping rooms, caring for girls aged 1–10 and boys aged 1–8 and handling around 1,500 children a day. For parents who want one rite — a quiet Tawaf, performed without a toddler in their arms — this is the official answer. Ask at the mosque's service points in the third expansion for the current intake arrangements when you arrive, as procedures are set by the mosque administration.

Age by age — what actually works:

  • Babies under 2: The easiest travellers of all, if you protect the routine. Carry them in a sling for the rites, time mosque visits around feeds and naps, and choose the quiet hours — after Fajr and late at night. Everything else is logistics: milk, nappies and a change of clothes for you as well as the baby, all in the bag you take into the mosque.
  • Toddlers 2–4: Mobile, curious and tireless at exactly the wrong moments. Keep Haram visits short and frequent rather than long; use the stroller everywhere it is allowed and the carrier where it is not; keep snacks and water on you; and expect to leave mid-plan sometimes — a calm exit beats a meltdown in the Mataf crowd.
  • School-age 5–12: Now it becomes their Umrah too. They need their own Nusuk permit; teach them the Talbiyah and a short dua before you travel; give them real jobs — carrying the Zamzam bottle, counting Tawaf circuits, holding the family's dua list. Most children this age can walk Sa'i at a gentle pace with breaks at the Zamzam points.
  • Teens 11+: Give them responsibility, not just instructions: navigating to the hotel, helping grandparents, keeping the group's documents photographed and backed up. A teen who is trusted with the trip's real work remembers it entirely differently from one who was simply brought along.

When to go with children: Heat is the family's real opponent. Makkah's summer passes 45°C, and small children dehydrate faster than adults and complain later. Aim for the mild months — roughly November to February — when Tawaf and the walk to the hotel stay comfortable for little legs; our Best Time to Perform Umrah guide maps weather, crowds and prices month by month. School holidays will pull you toward peak weeks; if you must travel then, lean harder on the quiet hours and the short-visit rhythm.

Where to stay: With children, distance is the whole decision. A hotel a short, flat walk from a Haram gate means a tired child is in bed ten minutes after leaving the mosque; a cheaper hotel across town means shuttle timetables ruling your prayers and your naps. Prioritise a family room, a fridge for milk and snacks, and breakfast timings that survive a Fajr start. Our Makkah Hotels Near Haram guide compares the areas by walking distance, and the same logic applies to your Madinah nights — the days there, planned with our Madinah Ziyarat guide, are usually the gentlest of the trip for children.

Between the two cities — the Haramain train with kids: The high-speed train is usually the family's best crossing: air-conditioned, spacious, and about two and a half hours against five on the road. On current published terms, children under 2 travel free on an adult's lap, and ages 2–11 travel at a discounted fare — but fares and child policies are revised, so confirm the current fare before booking. Book seats together, arrive with margin for the station taxi at each end, and keep the documents folder in hand luggage, never in the checked bags.

Packing checklist — the child-specific layer: Our Umrah Packing List covers the family's full list; add these for the children:

  • Soft carrier or sling, plus a compact foldable stroller.
  • ID wristbands with a parent's name and phone number.
  • Children's medicines, rehydration sachets and any prescription items, in hand luggage.
  • Snacks your children will actually eat, a refillable water bottle, wipes and nappy supplies for mosque-length outings.
  • A full change of clothes per child in the day bag — Zamzam spills and travel days guarantee the need.
  • Sun hat and light layers; mosque air-conditioning is cold on small bodies even when Makkah is hot.
  • One familiar comfort item from home, and a small dua book or audio duas for older children.

Quick questions:

  • Does my baby need their own visa and permit? Visa — yes: every child, including infants, needs their own passport and visa. Permit — only from age five: the Nusuk Umrah permit starts at five, and under-fives enter both mosques with their parents without a permit.
  • Can I take a stroller into the Grand Mosque? Not onto the ground-floor Mataf around the Kaaba, where strollers are banned for safety. They are generally permitted in the Mas'a corridor, on upper floors and in the outer courtyards. For Tawaf itself, use a soft carrier or sling.
  • Does my child's Umrah count, or must they repeat it? It counts as a valid, rewarded voluntary Umrah — the Prophet ﷺ confirmed the reward for the child and the parent alike (Sahih Muslim 1336a). It does not discharge the obligation, so they will perform Umrah again after puberty as their own duty.
