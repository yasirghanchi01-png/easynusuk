What to Pack for Umrah — Complete Packing List

Packing for Umrah is different from packing for an ordinary trip. You are dressing for worship as well as travel, walking long distances in heat, and entering Ihram on the way. This short list covers what you actually need — and what to leave behind — so you can focus on worship, not luggage.

1. Documents — in hand luggage, never in checked bags. If your suitcase is delayed, you still need to be able to travel and check in. Keep everything in one zipped pouch in your carry-on:

  • Passport valid for 6+ months from your travel date
  • Umrah visa — printed copy plus the PDF saved on your phone
  • Nusuk permit — saved as a screenshot in case the signal is weak
  • Flight tickets and hotel confirmations for Makkah and Madinah
  • Meningitis ACWY vaccination certificate — the vaccine must be taken at least 10 days before travel
  • Photocopies of everything, kept separately from the originals, plus digital copies on your phone

2. Men — Ihram essentials. Pack 2 Ihram sets in soft cotton or towel fabric — one will get sweaty or dusty, and a clean change is genuinely useful. Add an Ihram belt with pockets (Ihram has no pockets, yet you will be carrying your phone, cash and hotel key), and open sandals that leave the ankles uncovered. For toiletries, pack unscented soap, shampoo and deodorant — no perfume or scented products once you are in Ihram. Pack one full Ihram set in your hand luggage: if you are flying towards Jeddah, you may need to be in Ihram before the plane passes the miqat, and your suitcase will be in the hold.

3. Women — modest, breathable clothing. Women do not wear a special Ihram garment — normal modest clothing is worn. Pack 3–4 loose, breathable abayas and 4–5 hijabs in lightweight cotton or jersey that are easy to wash and manage through long days. While in Ihram, do not wear a niqab (face veil) or gloves.

4. Health — medicines done properly. Carry your regular medicines in their original packaging, in hand luggage, with prescriptions and a doctor's letter in case you are asked about them. Add basic pain relief and stomach medicine for headaches, fever and an upset stomach. If you take controlled drugs, you need an SFDA (Saudi Food and Drug Authority) permit arranged in advance through its traveller's permit system — do not leave this to the airport.

5. Money — riyals, a card, and split it up. Carry some Saudi riyals in cash plus at least one bank card, and split your money between bags — some in your hand luggage, some in a money belt — rather than keeping it all in one place. Travelling from Pakistan? The State Bank of Pakistan limit is US$5,000 in cash per visit for adults, so plan larger payments by card.

6. Electronics. Saudi Arabia uses Type G plugs at 220–230V — the same three-pin type as the UK and Pakistan — so pack a Type G adapter if your plugs differ. Bring a power bank (in hand luggage only) and your phone with the Nusuk app installed and set up before you fly — you will need it for permits from day one.

7. Day bag and Zamzam. Take a small day bag or crossbody bag for the Haram — for your phone, water bottle, shoe bag and any medication, leaving your hands free during Tawaf and Sa'i. On the way home, you are allowed one sealed 5L Zamzam bottle on the return flight, bought at the departure airport — never pack loose Zamzam bottles inside your suitcase.

8. What NOT to pack. Leave behind scented toiletries for your Ihram days (perfume, fragranced soap, shampoo and deodorant are not permitted in Ihram), valuables you do not need such as jewellery — one less thing to worry about losing in the crowds — and excess luggage. Makkah and Madinah have pharmacies and shops on every corner; pack light and leave room for what you buy there.

The carry-on rule: If you would need it in the first 24 hours, it goes in the carry-on — documents, medicines, one full Ihram set, sandals, chargers and one change of clothes. Everything else can go in the checked bag.
