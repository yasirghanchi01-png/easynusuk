Taking Zamzam Water Home: Flight Rules, the 5-Litre Allowance and Airport Collection

Almost every pilgrim wants to bring Zamzam water home for family. The good news is that Saudi rules allow every passenger one factory sealed 5-litre container of Zamzam. The rules are strict about the container, where it travels and which airline you fly, so get them right before check-in.

The one rule that matters most: only the official sealed container. Airlines accept only the factory sealed 5-litre box from the King Abdullah Bin Abdulaziz Zamzam Project. Buy it at the official counters at Jeddah or Madinah airport, for about SAR 12.50. Hotel filled bottles, loose cans and decorative flasks are refused at check-in. Never pack Zamzam inside your suitcase. Airlines forbid it as a safety rule, because a leak can soak everything you own and damage the aircraft. The sealed box travels on its own as a separate checked piece, with a Zamzam luggage tag attached at the counter.

Checked baggage only, never in the cabin. Zamzam never travels in cabin baggage. The worldwide 100 ml liquid rule applies on every airline, and a 5-litre container is fifty times that limit. Security screeners make no exceptions at the gate, so do not carry even a small bottle of Zamzam in your hand luggage. Put it through as checked baggage and collect it at the oversize baggage belt at your destination.

How much can you take. The Saudi allowance is one 5-litre container per passenger. A family of four can carry four containers, one for each person, including children travelling on their own tickets. A second bottle for the same passenger counts as excess baggage and is charged at normal excess rates. Rules change, so confirm the allowance with your airline before you travel, and remember that the allowance printed on your ticket overrides every table on the internet.

What the airlines actually allow. The rules differ by airline, and there are two kinds of free. On Saudia, one factory sealed 5-litre container is accepted per passenger as checked baggage. From Madinah, the sealed pack is accepted as a complimentary checked item. From Jeddah and Madinah it travels free of charge but it is counted as part of your checked baggage allowance, not added on top of it, so keep about 5 kg of your allowance spare for it. Jeddah Terminal 1 is excluded from the standard rule, so confirm at JED check-in. From other Saudi airports such as Riyadh and Dammam, the pack rides inside your free checked allowance, with excess charged at normal rates. Emirates, Etihad, Qatar Airways, Gulf Air, flydubai, Oman Air, Air India, Air India Express, SpiceJet and flynas fly one 5-litre container free on top of your baggage allowance in at least some cases. flynas lifted its Jeddah ban in May 2026 and asks for proof of Umrah in the Nusuk app. Saudia, IndiGo, Kuwait Airways and Akasa carry it inside your checked allowance. No airline in the checked data bans Zamzam from Jeddah any longer. If Zamzam is lost or delayed, Saudia pays no compensation and offers a replacement when possible.

At the airport, step by step. Buy the sealed box at the official Zamzam counter in the terminal before check-in. At the check-in desk, hand it over as a separate piece and make sure the agent attaches the Zamzam tag. Keep your boarding pass and the baggage receipt. At arrival, look for the oversize or special baggage belt, not the normal carousel. Open the box only when you are home. If you are transiting through another country, check that country's liquid and customs rules too, because some airports re-screen transit baggage.
