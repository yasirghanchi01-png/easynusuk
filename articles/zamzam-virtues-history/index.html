Zamzam Water — Virtues, History & How to Drink It

Every pilgrim drinks it, and most carry a bottle of it home like treasure: Zamzam, the water of the well beside the Kaaba. Its story begins with Hajar (AS), left in the empty valley of Makkah with her infant son Ismail (AS). When the baby cried with thirst, she ran between Safa and Marwa searching for water — the walk pilgrims still repeat in Sa'i — until, by Allah's mercy, water gushed from the ground at the child's feet, and she held it back, saying "Zam, zam" — "hold, hold" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3364). That spring has flowed ever since. If you are preparing for your first journey, our Umrah for First-Timers guide shows where Zamzam fits in the order of the rites.

Its virtues: The Prophet ﷺ said: "The water of Zamzam is for whatever it is drunk for" (Sunan Ibn Majah 3062). He ﷺ also said of it: "It is blessed; it is food that nourishes" (Sahih Muslim 2473a, narrated by Abu Dharr RA), and: "The best water on the face of the earth is the water of Zamzam" (Sahih al-Jami' 3302).

A note on the famous hadith, honestly: Sunan Ibn Majah 3062 is graded hasan in the Darussalam edition, but scholars have differed over its grading across the centuries. So take it the way the scholars advise: as a strong encouragement to drink Zamzam with a sincere intention — for healing, for knowledge, for provision, for whatever good you ask — not as a guaranteed promise that works by itself. Pair the intention with du'a, and leave the outcome to Allah.

How to drink it, the Sunnah way:

  • Face the Qibla if you can, and say Bismillah.
  • Drink in three sips, pausing to breathe between them — do not drain the cup in a single breath.
  • Drink your fill, until you are properly satisfied — not just a token sip.
  • Praise Allah afterwards (Alhamdulillah), then make your du'a.
  • Sprinkle some on your head, face and chest, as the Prophet ﷺ did with Zamzam.

The du'a of Ibn Abbas (RA): Whenever he drank Zamzam, Ibn Abbas (RA) would say: "Allahumma inni as'aluka 'ilman nafi'an, wa rizqan wasi'an, wa shifa'an min kulli da'" — "O Allah, I ask You for beneficial knowledge, plentiful provision, and healing from every disease" (recorded in IslamQA fatwa 1698). Use his words, or ask in your own. Many pilgrims pause for Zamzam straight after Tawaf, while their du'as are still warm.

Taking Zamzam home: Sealed Zamzam is sold at official sales points at Jeddah and Madinah airports for departing pilgrims. Under the Saudi civil aviation (GACA) rule, a pilgrim travelling on an Umrah or Hajj visa may take one sealed 5-litre bottle, which travels as checked baggage, handled separately — never pack loose bottles inside a suitcase, as airlines do not accept them. Whether that bottle is carried free or counts inside your normal allowance differs by airline and airport, so confirm with your airline before you fly. Our Umrah Packing List covers the rest of the journey-home luggage rules, and Hajj 2027 covers the same allowance in Hajj season.

Quick questions:

  • Can I drink Zamzam with an intention? Yes — that is exactly what the hadith encourages. Drink it intending a good you are asking Allah for — healing, knowledge, provision — and make du'a as you drink.
  • Is Zamzam mentioned in the Quran by name? No. The well is not named in the Qur'an; its story and virtues come to us from the Sunnah, above all Sahih al-Bukhari 3364 and Sahih Muslim 2473a.
  • Can I take Zamzam home? Yes. Buy the sealed 5-litre bottle at the official airport sales point when you depart; it is checked in separately under the Zamzam allowance. Loose bottles inside a suitcase are not accepted.
